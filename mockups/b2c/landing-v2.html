FAQ

Common questions

Everything employees ask us before getting started.

"Severance" is commonly used to mean everything you're owed on termination. In Ontario that's two layers: the Employment Standards Act minimum (notice under s. 57 and, for qualifying employers, severance pay under s. 64), and your common law reasonable notice, which is usually much larger and set by the Bardal factors.
It depends on your age, length of service, the type of role, and how easily you can find comparable work. A common rule of thumb is roughly a month of notice per year of service, but it's capped and fact-specific. Our free assessment gives you a realistic range — for example, an $85,000 manager with 8 years' service might see 9–12 months.
Often it isn't. Under Waksdale v Swegon Power & Cooling Systems Inc., 2020 ONCA 391, if any part of your termination clause violates the ESA, the entire clause can be void — which means you default to the much larger common law notice. We check this automatically.
Generally two years from your termination date to start a wrongful dismissal claim (the Limitations Act, 2002). A separate one-year deadline applies to a Human Rights Tribunal of Ontario application. Don't wait — missing a deadline can extinguish your claim.
Rarely right away. Employers often present the statutory minimum with a short deadline. You're usually free to negotiate, and signing a release gives up your right to claim more. Run a free assessment first so you know what you're giving up.

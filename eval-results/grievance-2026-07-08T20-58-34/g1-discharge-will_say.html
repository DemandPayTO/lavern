    • Notes for Union Representative

    Critical Gaps Requiring Immediate Attention

    The intake information contains significant gaps that must be filled before these will-say statements can be finalized and before the grievance proceeds to arbitration. The following information must be obtained from the witnesses:

    1. The Grievor (Claude Boisvert): The grievor's will-say contains numerous bracketed items requiring confirmation. Most critically, the union must obtain the grievor's detailed account of what actually happened on June 24, 2026, what the alleged falsification was, and what his explanation is. Without this information, the union cannot properly prepare the case or assess its merits.
    2. The Steward (Devon Price): The steward's will-say requires confirmation of what was said during the four-minute disciplinary meeting. This is critical evidence of procedural unfairness. The steward should also be asked whether any investigation meeting occurred before the disciplinary meeting and whether the grievor had union representation at any such meeting.
    3. The Coworker (Alia Rahman): The coworker's will-say is almost entirely bracketed because the intake does not specify what she observed. The union must interview her to determine what relevant observations she can provide about the grievor's work on June 24, 2026, or about his work practices and reputation generally.

    Procedural Issues Requiring Immediate Action

    1. Grievance Filing Deadline: The intake indicates the grievance has not yet been filed. Article 11 of the collective agreement imposes a 10-working-day deadline, which expires on July 8, 2026. The grievance must be filed immediately to preserve the union's right to arbitrate.
    2. Mandatory Time Limits: The collective agreement states that time limits are mandatory. Failure to file by July 8, 2026, will likely result in the grievance being dismissed as untimely. This is a critical deadline.

    Evidentiary Issues

    1. Lockout Verification Record: The union must obtain a copy of the lockout verification record from June 24, 2026, and any related documents. This is the central piece of evidence in the case. The union should also obtain copies of other lockout verification records completed by the grievor to establish his usual practice.
    2. Investigation Records: The union should request copies of any investigation notes, witness statements, or other documents created by the employer before the discharge decision. The intake indicates no investigation was conducted, but the union should confirm this and obtain any documents that exist.
    3. Disciplinary Records: The union must obtain a copy of the 2019 written warning and confirm whether any other discipline exists on the grievor's file. The union should also confirm the exact date of the 2019 warning to determine whether it falls outside the 24-month sunset period.
    4. Training Records: The union should obtain copies of any training records showing that the grievor was trained on lockout procedures and safety documentation requirements.
    5. Collective Agreement: The union must have a complete copy of the 2023–2026 collective agreement, particularly Article 5.02 (just cause), Article 11 (grievance procedure), and any articles dealing with safety, discipline, and sunset clauses.

    Legal Issues to Consider

    1. Just Cause: The employer bears the burden of proving just cause for discharge. The union should be prepared to argue that the employer has not met this burden, particularly given the lack of investigation, the brevity of the disciplinary meeting, and the grievor's 18 years of service.
    2. Procedural Fairness: The union has strong arguments regarding procedural unfairness: no proper investigation, no union representation during any investigation meeting, and a four-minute disciplinary meeting that did not give the grievor a meaningful opportunity to respond. These procedural defects may be sufficient to overturn the discharge even if the employer can prove the underlying misconduct.
    3. Progressive Discipline: The grievor has only one prior warning from 2019, which is outside the sunset period and was for an unrelated matter (late reporting, not safety). The union should argue that discharge is excessive and that the employer failed to apply progressive discipline.
    4. Mitigation: Even if the arbitrator finds that the grievor engaged in some misconduct, the union should argue for mitigation based on the grievor's long service, limited disciplinary record, and the procedural unfairness in how the discharge was imposed.
    5. Duty of Fair Representation: The intake lists duty of fair representation exposure as an approved issue. The union must ensure that it conducts a thorough investigation, properly prepares the case, and vigorously represents the grievor at arbitration. Any failure to do so could expose the union to a duty of fair representation complaint.

    Remedy

    The union is seeking reinstatement with full back pay, benefits, and seniority. The estimated back pay is $96,000. The union should be prepared to provide evidence of the grievor's wage rate, benefits, and any mitigation efforts (such as attempts to find other work or actual earnings from other employment during the period of discharge).

    1. File the grievance immediately to meet the July 8, 2026 deadline.
    2. Interview all witnesses to complete the bracketed portions of these will-say statements.
    3. Obtain all relevant documents from the employer and the union's files.

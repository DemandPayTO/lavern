1. Overview

The grievor, Claude Boisvert, is a Millwright in the Maintenance department at the Trillium Paper Products Inc Cornwall mill with just over 18 years of continuous, effectively unblemished service. On 24 June 2026 the Employer alleged that the grievor falsified a lockout verification record on a single occasion, and by letter dated 26 June 2026 it discharged him. The Employer conducted no investigation before imposing discipline, denied the grievor union representation at the disciplinary meeting, and terminated a career of 18 years in a meeting lasting four minutes. The Union seeks a declaration that the Employer lacked just cause, reinstatement with full back pay, benefits, pension, and seniority, and, in the alternative, substitution of a lesser penalty.

2. The Parties and the Collective Agreement

The Union is the certified bargaining agent for the employees at the Employer's Cornwall mill, including Maintenance employees in the Millwright classification. The parties are governed by the 2023–2026 Collective Agreement (the "Agreement").

The grievance procedure is set out in Article 11 of the Agreement. The time limits within Article 11 are expressly stated to be mandatory, a matter addressed further below because it bears equally on both parties.

The disciplinary standard is governed by Article 5.02 of the Agreement (the just cause provision). Where the specific text of Article 5.02 is available on the intake record it should be quoted verbatim here; it is not reproduced in the intake data provided and must be inserted before filing. The Union's position throughout rests on the requirement that the Employer establish just cause for discharge.

  5. The 2019 written warning is well beyond the 24-month sunset period and is therefore inactive and unavailable to the Employer as a basis for or aggravating factor in the discharge.

  7. The Employer conducted no investigation before imposing discipline.
  8. The grievor was not afforded union representation at the disciplinary meeting.
  9. The disciplinary meeting lasted approximately four minutes.
  10. By letter dated 26 June 2026, the Employer discharged the grievor. The stated ground was falsification of safety documentation.
  11. The Union's witnesses will include the grievor, who will testify to the incident, the meeting, and his record; Devon Price, Steward, who will testify to the disciplinary meeting and the supervisor's statements; and Alia Rahman, a coworker, who will testify to what she observed on the floor before the incident.

4. Issues

  1. Did the Employer have just cause to discharge the grievor?
  2. If not, what is the appropriate remedy, and should the grievor be reinstated and made whole?
  3. Do the Employer's failure to investigate and its denial of union representation constitute procedural defects that vitiate or undermine the discipline?
  4. Is the 2019 written warning available to the Employer given the 24-month sunset clause?
  5. Are the mandatory time limits in Article 11 satisfied, and what is the consequence for the arbitrability of the grievance?

5. Argument

Just Cause: The William Scott Framework

The onus rests on the Employer to establish just cause on the balance of probabilities. The well-established analytical framework asks three questions: (a) did the employee give the Employer cause for discipline; (b) if so, was discharge an excessive response in the circumstances; and (c) if so, what alternative measure should be substituted as just and equitable.

On the first question, an allegation of falsification of a safety record is serious, but seriousness is not proof. The Employer bears the burden of proving that the grievor deliberately and dishonestly falsified the lockout verification record. The intake discloses a single occasion, an allegation, and no investigation. Absent an investigation, the Employer has no reliable evidentiary foundation to distinguish deliberate falsification from error, misunderstanding, or a legitimate account of what occurred on the floor. The evidence of Alia Rahman regarding conditions on the floor before the incident, and of the grievor himself, is directly relevant to whether any misconduct in fact occurred.

On the second question, even if some culpable conduct is proven, discharge is a grossly excessive response. Discipline must be proportionate. The relevant mitigating factors are compelling: 18 years of service, an effectively clean record (the sole prior warning being both minor and time-barred), a single alleged incident, and the absence of any prior progressive discipline for anything comparable. Arbitrators consistently treat long service with a clean record as among the strongest mitigating factors weighing against discharge.

On the third question, the appropriate and just outcome is reinstatement, with any lesser penalty substituted only if some culpable conduct is established.

Procedural Defects

The manner in which the Employer proceeded is fatally deficient in two respects. First, the Employer conducted no investigation before imposing discipline. A fair and adequate investigation is a fundamental component of the disciplinary process; the failure to investigate deprives the Employer of any reliable basis for the conclusion that misconduct occurred and denies the grievor any opportunity to respond before the decision was made.

Second, the grievor was denied union representation at the disciplinary meeting. A meeting that ends the career of an 18-year employee in four minutes, without representation and without prior investigation, is the antithesis of a fair process. These defects independently weigh heavily against upholding the discharge and are relevant both to the just cause analysis and to remedy.

Prior Record Challenge: The Sunset Clause

The Agreement provides that discipline is removed after 24 months. The grievor's only prior discipline is a 2019 written warning for late reporting. That warning is well outside the 24-month window and is inactive. The Employer may not rely on it to establish a disciplinary history, to characterise the grievor as a repeat offender, or as an aggravating factor. For all disciplinary purposes the grievor stands before this arbitration board with a clean record.

Time Limits Under Article 11

The time limits in Article 11 are expressly mandatory. The grievance filing deadline is 10 working days, expiring 8 July 2026. The Union must ensure the grievance is filed within that deadline; failure to do so would expose the grievance to a preliminary objection as to arbitrability. Counsel is directed to confirm the grievance has been filed in time before any hearing. Mandatory time limits cut both ways: where the Employer has failed to meet any mandatory obligation of its own under Article 11, the Union reserves the right to raise that failure.

Exclusive Forum

This dispute arises expressly out of the interpretation, application, and administration of the Agreement, namely the discharge and the just cause provision at Article 5.02. It falls squarely within the exclusive jurisdiction of this arbitration board as the forum for resolution of differences under the Agreement. There is no basis for the matter to proceed elsewhere; the arbitration process is the proper and exclusive forum for the remedies sought.

Duty of Fair Representation

The Union is alive to its obligations to the grievor. Given that the grievance had not yet been filed at the date of preparation and that the mandatory Article 11 deadline of 8 July 2026 governs, prompt and diligent processing of this grievance is essential. This note is included so that the responsible officer confirms timely filing and diligent carriage; no admission is made and none should be read into this brief.

6. Remedy

The presumptive remedy for a discharge found to be without just cause is reinstatement with full make-whole compensation. The Union seeks:

  1. Reinstatement to the grievor's position as Millwright, Maintenance, without loss of seniority (seniority date 17 March 2008);
  2. Full back pay from the date of discharge to the date of reinstatement, estimated at approximately $96,000 and to be quantified precisely;
  3. Restoration of all benefits and pension contributions and credits for the period of discharge;
  4. Interest on all monetary amounts;
  5. Removal of the discharge and all reference to it from the grievor's record.

In the alternative, if some culpable conduct is established, the Union seeks substitution of a lesser penalty proportionate to the proven conduct, having regard to the grievor's 18 years of service, his clean and time-barred record, the single alleged incident, and the serious procedural defects in the Employer's process.

7. Order Requested

  1. A declaration that the Employer did not have just cause to discharge the grievor;
  2. An order reinstating the grievor to his position without loss of seniority;
  3. An order for full back pay estimated at approximately $96,000, to be quantified;
  4. An order restoring all benefits, pension contributions, and credits;
  5. An order for interest on all monetary amounts;
  6. An order that the discharge and all reference to it be removed from the grievor's record;
  7. In the alternative, substitution of a lesser penalty proportionate to any proven conduct;
  8. An order that the arbitrator remain seized to resolve any dispute regarding implementation of the remedy.

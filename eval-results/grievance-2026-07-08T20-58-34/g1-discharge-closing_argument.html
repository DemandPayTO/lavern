Closing Argument Skeleton

Grievor: Claude Boisvert, Millwright, Maintenance Department (seniority date 17 March 2008)

Union: IAM Local 235

Employer: Trillium Paper Products Inc (Cornwall mill)

Nature of grievance: Discharge dated 26 June 2026, arising from an incident of 24 June 2026, on the stated ground of falsification of safety documentation.

Prepared by: Jordan Whitfield, Whitfield Labour Law

Status: DRAFT for review by union counsel or the responsible labour relations officer before use. Evidence slots remain open pending completion of the hearing.

Overview and Order of Argument

This is a discharge grievance. The governing analytical framework is the three part inquiry set out in Re Wm. Scott and Company Ltd., [1977] 1 Can LRBR 1. The Union will argue, first, whether the Employer has established just and reasonable cause for any discipline; second, if some cause is established, whether discharge was an excessive response; and third, if discharge was excessive, what alternative measure is just and equitable. The Union submits that the Employer fails at the first stage, and that even if some misconduct is found, discharge is a grossly disproportionate response to a single alleged incident by an employee with eighteen years of service. The Union addresses several threshold and procedural matters below and reserves remedy for last.

Issue 1: Just Cause Challenge

The proposition

The Employer bears the onus of proving, on clear and cogent evidence, that Claude Boisvert falsified a lockout verification record and that this conduct justified discipline. The Union submits that the Employer has not discharged that onus.

The anchor

Article 5.02 (just cause). The burden of proof rests on the Employer in every discipline and discharge case, and the standard is proof on a balance of probabilities supported by clear and cogent evidence, given the seriousness of an allegation of dishonesty.

Supporting facts

  1. The Employer alleges falsification on a single occasion only. [EVIDENCE: the specific record alleged to have been falsified, and the entry said to be false]
  2. An allegation of falsification requires proof of intent to deceive, not merely an error or omission. [EVIDENCE: Claude Boisvert evidence as to what he recorded, what he verified, and his understanding of the procedure]
  3. Coworker Alia Rahman observed the floor before the incident and can speak to the actual conditions and sequence of events. [EVIDENCE: Rahman testimony on what she observed on the floor before the incident]
  4. No investigation was conducted before the discipline was imposed, so the Employer's factual foundation was never tested at the time. [EVIDENCE: confirmation from supervisor in cross that no investigation occurred]

Anticipated employer position and the Union's answer

The Employer will argue that falsification of safety documentation is inherently serious and strikes at the trust relationship. The Union answers that seriousness in the abstract does not relieve the Employer of proving the specific act and the specific intent. Where the alleged conduct is a single entry, and where honest error, misunderstanding, or a genuine verification supported by contemporaneous observation is a live possibility, the Employer must exclude those innocent explanations. The Union submits it has not. [AUTHORITY: arbitral authority on the requirement of proof of intent to deceive in falsification cases]

Issue 2: Grievance Time Limits

The proposition

The grievance is timely and properly before the arbitrator. The mandatory time limits in Article 11 do not bar this grievance.

The anchor

Article 11 (grievance procedure); the filing deadline of ten working days. The discipline letter is dated 26 June 2026 and the filing deadline is 8 July 2026.

Supporting facts

  1. The Union must establish that the grievance was filed within ten working days of the event giving rise to it. [EVIDENCE: date the grievance was in fact filed, and proof of service on the Employer]
  2. If any objection to timeliness is raised, the Union relies on the calculation of working days and the date of receipt of the discharge letter. [EVIDENCE: date Claude Boisvert received the letter dated 26 June 2026]

Anticipated employer position and the Union's answer

Should the Employer assert that the time limits are mandatory and that any delay bars the grievance, the Union answers that the grievance was filed within the prescribed period. In the alternative, the Union relies on section 48(16) of the Labour Relations Act, 1995, which empowers the arbitrator to extend time limits where there are reasonable grounds for the extension and the opposite party is not substantially prejudiced. Given the significance of the interest at stake, a discharge terminating eighteen years of service, no substantial prejudice arises from any minor irregularity. [AUTHORITY: authority on relief against time limits under section 48(16)]

Issue 3: Weber and the Exclusive Forum

The proposition

This dispute lies squarely within the exclusive jurisdiction of this arbitration board. The essential character of the dispute arises from the interpretation, application, and administration of the Collective Agreement.

The anchor

Weber v. Ontario Hydro, [1995] 2 SCR 929, and section 48 of the Labour Relations Act, 1995. The subject matter is discipline and discharge under Article 5.02, which is the heartland of an arbitrator's jurisdiction.

Supporting facts

  1. The dispute concerns the termination of employment under a collective agreement containing a just cause standard. [EVIDENCE: the discharge letter and its grounds]

Anticipated employer position and the Union's answer

The Union does not anticipate a genuine jurisdictional dispute; the matter is included here for completeness. To the extent that any question of forum is raised, the arbitrator has exclusive jurisdiction over the entire dispute in its factual matrix, and there is no competing forum that displaces arbitration.

Issue 4: Duty of Fair Representation Exposure

The proposition

This issue is noted to confirm that the Union has carried out its representational obligations diligently and that no duty of fair representation concern arises from its handling of the file.

The anchor

Section 74 of the Labour Relations Act, 1995. The Union's conduct must not be arbitrary, discriminatory, or in bad faith.

Supporting facts

  1. The Union has advanced the grievance to arbitration and has assembled witnesses including the Grievor, the Steward, and a coworker. [EVIDENCE: record of the Union's steps in advancing the grievance]
  2. The Steward, Devon Price, was present in the disciplinary process and can address the Union's involvement. [EVIDENCE: Price testimony on the Union's response to the discipline]

Anticipated employer position and the Union's answer

No employer position is expected on this internal matter. This heading records for the file that the grievance was pursued vigorously and on the merits, and that no representational deficiency exists.

Issue 5: Prior Record Challenge

The proposition

The Grievor's disciplinary record is effectively clean and cannot support the escalation to discharge. The single prior warning is stale and must be disregarded.

The anchor

Article 5.02 and the sunset clause providing that discipline is removed from the record after twenty four months.

Supporting facts

  1. The only prior discipline is a written warning in 2019 for late reporting, which was not grieved. [EVIDENCE: the 2019 written warning document and its date]
  2. The applicable sunset period is twenty four months. A warning from 2019 falls well outside that window and cannot be relied upon for any purpose in 2026. [EVIDENCE: confirmation of the sunset provision and its terms]
  3. The Grievor has therefore no active discipline on his record, and eighteen years of otherwise unblemished service. [EVIDENCE: Claude Boisvert evidence on his work history and any commendations]

Anticipated employer position and the Union's answer

If the Employer seeks to characterise the discharge as the culmination of a pattern, the Union answers that there is no pattern. A single expired warning for an unrelated matter is not a foundation for progressive discipline. This discharge stands or falls on the single incident alone, and the record supports leniency rather than severity.

Issue 6: Procedural Defects

The proposition

The disciplinary process was fundamentally deficient. The absence of any investigation and the absence of union representation at the disciplinary meeting are serious defects that undermine the reliability and fairness of the discharge decision.

The anchor

Article 5.02 and Article 11, together with the general arbitral requirement of a fair and adequate investigation before serious discipline is imposed.

Supporting facts

  1. No investigation was conducted before the discipline was imposed. The Employer reached a conclusion of falsification without gathering or testing the relevant facts. [EVIDENCE: supervisor's admission or the absence of any investigation notes or interviews]
  2. The Grievor had no union representation at the disciplinary meeting. [EVIDENCE: Claude Boisvert and Price testimony confirming no representative was present]
  3. The disciplinary meeting lasted only four minutes. This brevity demonstrates that no meaningful opportunity was given to the Grievor to respond to the allegation. [EVIDENCE: Claude Boisvert and Price testimony on the duration and content of the meeting]
  4. The Steward, Devon Price, can testify to what the supervisor said during the process. [EVIDENCE: Price testimony on the supervisor's statements and the conduct of the meeting]

Anticipated employer position and the Union's answer

The Employer may argue that procedural defects are cured by the hearing before the arbitrator, at which the Grievor has a full opportunity to respond. The Union answers that a de novo hearing does not neutralise every procedural failing, particularly where the failure to investigate means the Employer imposed discharge on an untested and possibly mistaken factual basis. The complete absence of investigation, combined with a four minute meeting and the denial of representation, is powerful evidence that the decision was arbitrary and premature. These defects are, at minimum, significant mitigating factors going to the appropriateness of the penalty. [AUTHORITY: arbitral authority on the requirement of a fair investigation and the treatment of procedural defects]

Mitigating Factors

The Union relies on the following mitigating factors, argued specifically and not merely listed.

Length and quality of service. The Grievor has eighteen years of seniority, from 17 March 2008. This is a substantial equity that weighs heavily against the ultimate penalty of discharge. [EVIDENCE: Claude Boisvert service history and any evidence of good performance]

Effectively clean record. The only prior discipline is a stale 2019 warning that has fallen outside the twenty four month sunset window. There is no active discipline against which to measure any progression. [EVIDENCE: confirmation the 2019 warning is the sole prior discipline]

Absence of any investigation. The Employer imposed the most serious penalty available without investigating the facts. The decision was therefore made without a reliable foundation and reflects a rush to judgment. [EVIDENCE: absence of investigation notes, interviews, or findings]

No union representation at the meeting. The Grievor faced the disciplinary meeting without a representative, and the meeting lasted only four minutes. He was denied a genuine opportunity to explain his conduct before the decision was taken. [EVIDENCE: Price and Claude Boisvert testimony on the meeting]

Single incident. The allegation concerns one occasion only. There is no suggestion of repeated or ongoing misconduct. [EVIDENCE: the Employer's own characterisation of the incident as a single occasion]

Issue 7: Reinstatement and Make Whole Remedy

The proposition

The discharge should be set aside in its entirety and the Grievor reinstated with full compensation. In the alternative, if any discipline is found warranted, a lesser penalty should be substituted.

The anchor

Article 5.02 and section 48(17) of the Labour Relations Act, 1995, which authorises the arbitrator to substitute such penalty as is just and reasonable in the circumstances.

The order sought

  1. A declaration that the Employer did not have just cause to discharge the Grievor.
  2. An order rescinding the discharge and reinstating the Grievor to his position as Millwright in the Maintenance Department, without loss of seniority.
  3. An order for full compensation for all lost wages, with the back pay estimated at $96,000 subject to proof, together with all lost benefits and pension entitlements. [EVIDENCE: calculation of lost earnings, benefits, and mitigation]
  4. An order that the compensation carry interest.
  5. An order that the Employer remove all reference to the discharge and the underlying allegation from the Grievor's record.
  6. An order that the arbitrator remain seized with respect to the implementation and calculation of the remedy.

Alternative remedy

If the arbitrator finds that some misconduct occurred and that some discipline was warranted, the Union submits that discharge was an excessive response under the second branch of Wm. Scott. Given eighteen years of service, an effectively clean record, a single incident, and the serious procedural defects in the process, the just and equitable measure under the third branch is substitution of a penalty well short of discharge, coupled with reinstatement and compensation for the period of any excess penalty. [AUTHORITY: authority on substitution of penalty and the relevance of long service and clean record]

Conclusion

The Employer imposed the capital penalty of employment on the basis of an uninvestigated, single incident allegation, after a four minute meeting at which the Grievor had no representation. The Employer has not proven the falsification it alleges, and even if some misconduct were found, discharge cannot survive scrutiny against a record of eighteen years of service and no active discipline. The Union asks that the grievance be allowed and that the Grievor be reinstated and made whole.

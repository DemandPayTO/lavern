# PARTICULARS OF THE GRIEVANCE **IN THE MATTER OF AN ARBITRATION** **BETWEEN:** **Trillium Paper Products Inc** (the "Employer") **- and -** **IAM Local 235** (the "Union") **RE: Discharge Grievance of Claude Boisvert** --- ## PRELIMINARY STATEMENT These particulars are provided without prejudice to the Union's right to rely on the full scope of the grievance, which is to be construed generously in accordance with established labour arbitration principles. The Union reserves the right to supplement or amend these particulars as further information becomes available through the disclosure process or otherwise. --- ## PARTICULARS 1. Claude Boisvert (the "Grievor") is a Millwright in the Maintenance department at the Employer's Cornwall mill. His seniority date is March 17, 2008. At all material times, he had approximately 18 years of service. 2. On or about June 24, 2026, the Grievor was working his regular shift in the Maintenance department. 3. On June 24, 2026, the Grievor was alleged to have falsified a lockout verification record on one occasion. The specific time, location, and circumstances of the alleged falsification are [TO BE CONFIRMED] pending full disclosure from the Employer. 4. Prior to June 24, 2026, no investigation meeting was conducted with the Grievor regarding the alleged incident. 5. On or about [TO BE CONFIRMED], the Grievor was summoned to a disciplinary meeting. 6. At the disciplinary meeting, the Grievor was not provided with union representation. Devon Price, a union steward, was present but [TO BE CONFIRMED: the nature and extent of Mr. Price's participation]. 7. The disciplinary meeting lasted approximately four minutes. 8. At the disciplinary meeting, the Employer did not conduct a meaningful investigation into the alleged incident, did not afford the Grievor a fair opportunity to respond to the allegations, and did not consider mitigating factors including the Grievor's 18 years of service and his disciplinary record. 9. By letter dated June 26, 2026, the Employer discharged the Grievor. The stated ground for discharge was "falsification of safety documentation." 10. The Grievor's prior disciplinary record consists of one written warning issued in 2019 for late reporting. That warning was not grieved. The Collective Agreement provides for a sunset period of 24 months for disciplinary records, and accordingly, the 2019 warning had expired and was no longer part of the Grievor's active disciplinary record at the time of the discharge. 11. The Employer did not conduct a proper investigation prior to imposing discipline. The Employer did not interview [TO BE CONFIRMED: witnesses or other employees who may have had relevant information]. The Employer did not review [TO BE CONFIRMED: relevant documentation, video footage, or other evidence that may have been available]. 12. The Employer failed to provide the Grievor with adequate notice of the case he had to meet at the disciplinary meeting. 13. The Employer failed to afford the Grievor procedural fairness in the investigation and disciplinary process. 14. The Employer failed to ensure that the Grievor had effective union representation at the disciplinary meeting. 15. The discharge was imposed without just cause. The Employer has not established that the Grievor engaged in the alleged misconduct. 16. In the alternative, if the Grievor engaged in any conduct that could be characterized as misconduct (which is not admitted), the Employer's response was excessive and disproportionate in all the circumstances, including: a) The Grievor's 18 years of unblemished service, save for a single expired warning; b) The absence of any prior discipline for similar conduct; c) The lack of a proper investigation; d) The denial of procedural fairness; e) The failure to consider mitigating factors; f) The failure to consider progressive discipline; and g) The minimal duration of the disciplinary meeting, which did not permit meaningful consideration of the Grievor's circumstances. 17. The discharge has caused the Grievor significant financial loss, loss of benefits, damage to his reputation, and emotional distress. --- ## VIOLATIONS OF THE COLLECTIVE AGREEMENT AND APPLICABLE LAW 18. By its actions and omissions as particularized above, the Employer has violated the following provisions of the 2023–2026 Collective Agreement: a) Article 5.02, which provides that the Employer may only discipline or discharge employees for just cause; and b) Such other provisions of the Collective Agreement as may be identified through the arbitration process. 19. The Employer has further violated the Grievor's rights under the common law of the employment relationship, including the duty of fairness in disciplinary proceedings, and such statutory rights as may be applicable. --- ## REMEDY SOUGHT 20. The Union seeks the following remedies: a) A declaration that the Grievor was discharged without just cause; b) An order that the Grievor be reinstated to his position as Millwright in the Maintenance department with full seniority and without loss of service; c) An order that the Grievor be made whole for all losses suffered as a result of the discharge, including: i) Full back pay from the date of discharge to the date of reinstatement; ii) All benefits, pension contributions, and other compensation that would have been earned or accrued during the period of discharge; iii) Interest on all monetary amounts owing; d) An order that the discharge and all references to it be expunged from the Grievor's employment record; e) Such further and other relief as the Union may request and as the Arbitrator may deem just and appropriate. --- **DATED** at [City], Ontario, this [day] of [month], 2026. **IAM Local 235** Per: ___________________________ Jordan Whitfield Whitfield Labour Law Counsel for the Union --- **DRAFT FOR REVIEW – NOT FOR FILING WITHOUT AUTHORIZATION**

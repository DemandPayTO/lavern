# PARTICULARS OF THE GRIEVANCE **IN THE MATTER OF AN ARBITRATION** **BETWEEN:** **IAM Local 235** (the "Union") **- and -** **Trillium Paper Products Inc** (the "Employer") **RE: Discharge Grievance of Claude Boisvert** --- ## PRELIMINARY STATEMENT These particulars are provided without prejudice to the Union's right to rely on the full scope of the grievance, which is to be construed generously in accordance with established labour arbitration principles. The Union reserves the right to supplement or amend these particulars as further information becomes available through the disclosure process or otherwise. --- ## PARTICULARS 1. Claude Boisvert (the "Grievor") was employed by the Employer as a Millwright in the Maintenance department at its Cornwall mill facility. 2. The Grievor's seniority date was March 17, 2008. At the time of her discharge, she had approximately 18 years of continuous service with the Employer. 3. On or about June 24, 2026, the Grievor was working her regular shift in the Maintenance department. 4. On June 24, 2026, the Grievor completed a lockout verification record in the course of her duties as a Millwright. 5. On or about [TO BE CONFIRMED], a management representative reviewed the lockout verification record completed by the Grievor on June 24, 2026. 6. On or about [TO BE CONFIRMED], the Employer formed the belief that the Grievor had falsified the lockout verification record dated June 24, 2026. 7. The Employer did not conduct a meaningful investigation into the circumstances surrounding the completion of the lockout verification record prior to imposing discipline. 8. On or about [TO BE CONFIRMED], the Grievor attended a meeting with management. The Union alleges that this meeting was investigatory in nature and that the Grievor was entitled to union representation at this meeting. 9. The Grievor was not provided with union representation at the investigatory meeting referenced in paragraph 8. 10. On or about June 26, 2026, the Grievor was directed to attend a meeting with management. 11. The meeting on June 26, 2026 lasted approximately four minutes. 12. At the meeting on June 26, 2026, the Employer informed the Grievor that she was discharged from employment. 13. The Employer's stated reason for the discharge was the alleged falsification of the lockout verification record on June 24, 2026. 14. The Employer provided the Grievor with a letter of discharge dated June 26, 2026. 15. The Grievor had one prior disciplinary notation on her record: a written warning issued in 2019 for late reporting to work. 16. The Grievor did not grieve the 2019 written warning. 17. The 2019 written warning was subject to a sunset clause of 24 months as provided in the collective agreement, and accordingly was no longer active discipline at the time of the discharge. 18. The alleged conduct on June 24, 2026 was a single, isolated incident. 19. The Employer failed to conduct a fair and thorough investigation before imposing discipline. 20. The Employer failed to provide the Grievor with union representation at meetings where she was entitled to such representation. 21. The discharge meeting on June 26, 2026 was perfunctory and did not afford the Grievor a meaningful opportunity to respond to the allegations against her. 22. The Employer failed to consider the Grievor's 18 years of service and her otherwise unblemished disciplinary record (having regard to the sunset provision). 23. The Employer failed to consider alternative disciplinary measures less severe than discharge. 24. The penalty of discharge was excessive, unreasonable, and disproportionate in all the circumstances. 25. The Employer did not have just cause to discipline or discharge the Grievor. --- ## VIOLATIONS OF THE COLLECTIVE AGREEMENT AND APPLICABLE LEGISLATION 26. By its conduct as particularized above, the Employer violated Article 5.02 of the collective agreement (just cause provision), Article 11 of the collective agreement (grievance procedure and related procedural rights), and such other provisions of the 2023-2026 collective agreement as may be applicable. The Employer further violated the Grievor's rights to fair representation and procedural fairness, and acted in a manner that was arbitrary, discriminatory, and in bad faith. The Employer's actions violated the principles of progressive discipline, proportionality, and the duty to conduct a fair investigation. The discharge was without just cause and constitutes an unjust dismissal. --- ## REMEDY SOUGHT 27. The Union seeks the following remedies: (a) A declaration that the Employer did not have just cause to discharge the Grievor; (b) An order that the Grievor be reinstated to her position as Millwright in the Maintenance department with full seniority and without loss of service; (c) An order that the Grievor be made whole for all losses suffered as a result of the unjust discharge, including but not limited to: (i) Full compensation for lost wages and benefits from the date of discharge to the date of reinstatement, estimated at approximately $96,000.00; (ii) Restoration of all benefits, pension contributions, and service credits; (iii) Compensation for any other pecuniary losses flowing from the discharge; (d) An order that the discharge letter and all references to this matter be removed from the Grievor's employment file; (e) Interest on all monetary awards; (f) Such further and other relief as the Union may request and as the Arbitrator deems just and appropriate. --- **DATED** at [City], Ontario, this [day] day of [month], 2026. **IAM Local 235** Per: ___________________________ Jordan Whitfield Whitfield Labour Law Counsel for the Union --- **DRAFT FOR REVIEW – NOT FOR FILING WITHOUT APPROVAL**

# DRAFT FOR REVIEW **DELIVERY:** [CONFIRM DELIVERY METHOD: REGISTERED MAIL / EMAIL / PERSONAL SERVICE] **DATE:** [INSERT DATE] **TO:** Marek Kowalski [INSERT ADDRESS] **FROM:** Teamsters Local 419 [INSERT UNION ADDRESS] **RE: Grievance #2026-419-008 – Discharge – Decision Not to Advance to Arbitration** --- Dear Marek Kowalski: I am writing to advise you of the union's decision regarding your grievance filed on February 6, 2026, concerning your discharge from employment with Bayfront Distribution Centres Inc on February 3, 2026. After careful consideration of all available information, the union has decided not to refer your grievance to arbitration. I understand this is not the outcome you hoped for, and I recognize the significant impact this decision has on you and your family. This letter explains the process the union followed, the reasons for the decision, and the options available to you. ## The Process The union has conducted a thorough review of your grievance. This review included: 1. **The grievance file**, including your written statement, the employer's discharge letter dated February 3, 2026, the investigation report, the post-incident drug test results, witness statements from the forklift collision, and all correspondence between you and the union; 2. **The collective agreement**, particularly Article 7 (grievance procedure), the provisions governing discipline and discharge, and any provisions relating to workplace safety and the employer's drug and alcohol policy; 3. **Your disciplinary record**, including the verbal warning issued in 2024 for a safety violation and the written warning issued in 2025 for a near-miss incident; 4. **The employer's drug and alcohol policy**, which sets out a zero-tolerance standard for impairment at work; 5. **A legal opinion** obtained from labour counsel experienced in discharge arbitrations, dated March 5, 2026, which assessed the merits of the grievance and the likelihood of success at arbitration; 6. **Comparable arbitration decisions** involving discharge for positive drug tests following workplace incidents, including cases addressing post-incident testing, the evidentiary value of drug test results, the application of zero-tolerance policies, and the relevance of prior safety-related discipline; and 7. **Review by the bargaining unit grievance committee** at its meeting on March 10, 2026, where your grievance was discussed and the recommendation not to advance to arbitration was made. Throughout this process, the union provided you with written updates on February 20, 2026, March 1, 2026, March 8, 2026, and March 15, 2026. You were invited to attend the grievance committee meeting on March 10, 2026, and you attended and presented your position to the committee. ## The Reasons for the Decision Your position, as you have consistently stated, is that you were not impaired at work on February 3, 2026, that the drug test detected cannabis you consumed off-duty several days earlier, that the test does not prove impairment, and that the discharge is unjust. You have also pointed to your seniority (since January 10, 2022) and your work record apart from the two prior safety warnings. The union has given careful consideration to your position. However, after reviewing all of the information, the union has concluded that the grievance has poor prospects of success at arbitration for the following reasons: **The Incident and the Test Results** On February 3, 2026, you were operating a forklift in the warehouse when you collided with a support column, causing damage to the forklift and the structure. The employer's investigation report includes statements from two coworkers who observed you immediately before and after the collision. Both witnesses reported that you appeared "out of it" and that your movements were slow and uncoordinated. The shift supervisor's statement indicates that when he spoke to you immediately after the collision, your speech was slow and you had difficulty focusing on his questions. Following the collision, the employer required you to undergo a post-incident drug test in accordance with its drug and alcohol policy. The test was conducted at an accredited laboratory on the same day. The test results, which are part of the employer's file, show the presence of THC (the psychoactive component of cannabis) at a level that the laboratory report describes as consistent with recent use. You have consistently maintained that you consumed cannabis off-duty on the weekend before the incident (January 31 or February 1, 2026) and that you were not impaired at work. You have pointed to the fact that THC can remain detectable in the body for days or weeks after use, and that the test does not measure impairment at a specific point in time. This is a scientifically accurate statement, and it is an argument that has been raised in arbitration cases. However, the legal opinion obtained by the union notes that arbitrators have upheld discharges for positive post-incident drug tests where the employer can point to observable signs of impairment in addition to the test results. In this case, the employer has witness statements describing behaviour consistent with impairment (slow movements, difficulty focusing, uncoordinated actions) in addition to the positive test result and the fact of the collision itself. The legal opinion assesses that an arbitrator would likely find that the employer had reasonable grounds to conclude that you were impaired at the time of the incident. **The Employer's Policy** The employer's drug and alcohol policy, which is referenced in the collective agreement and was reviewed during your orientation in January 2022, sets out a zero-tolerance standard for impairment at work. The policy provides that employees who are found to be impaired at work, or who test positive for drugs or alcohol following a workplace incident, are subject to discipline up to and including discharge. You have argued that the policy is unreasonable because it does not distinguish between impairment and the mere presence of THC in the body. The union raised this argument in the Step 2 meeting on March 12, 2026. The employer's response was that the policy is applied in conjunction with observable evidence of impairment, and that in your case, the test result was considered together with the witness observations and the fact of the collision. The legal opinion notes that arbitrators have generally upheld employer drug and alcohol policies that prohibit impairment at work, provided the policies are clearly communicated, consistently applied, and reasonable in the circumstances. The opinion assesses that an arbitrator would likely find the employer's policy to be reasonable, particularly in a workplace where employees operate forklifts and other heavy equipment, and where safety is a paramount concern. **Your Prior Disciplinary Record** Your disciplinary record includes a verbal warning issued in 2024 for a safety violation (the file indicates this was for failing to wear required personal protective equipment in a designated area) and a written warning issued in 2025 for a near-miss incident (the file indicates you were operating a forklift and came within inches of striking a coworker who was walking in the aisle). Both warnings reference the employer's safety policies and the expectation that you would exercise greater care in the future. You have argued that these prior warnings are not sufficiently serious to support a discharge, and that the employer should have considered progressive discipline (such as a suspension) rather than terminating your employment. The union raised this argument at Step 2. However, the legal opinion notes that arbitrators have recognized that employers in safety-sensitive workplaces are entitled to take a strict approach to discipline for safety violations, particularly where an employee has been previously warned. The opinion assesses that an arbitrator would likely find that the prior warnings, combined with the seriousness of the February 3, 2026 incident (a collision while operating a forklift, with evidence of impairment), provided the employer with just cause for discharge. The opinion notes that while progressive discipline is the general rule, arbitrators have upheld discharges without prior suspensions where the misconduct is sufficiently serious and where the employee has been previously warned about similar conduct. **The Prospects at Arbitration** Taking all of these factors into account, the legal opinion concludes that the grievance has poor prospects of success at arbitration. The opinion assesses that an arbitrator would likely find that the employer had just cause for discharge, and that the grievance would be dismissed. The opinion notes that there is a possibility that an arbitrator might substitute a lengthy suspension for the discharge, but assesses this outcome as unlikely given the evidence of impairment, the safety-sensitive nature of the work, and your prior disciplinary record. ## What the Union Weighed In deciding whether to refer a grievance to arbitration, the union must weigh a number of factors. These include: 1. **The merits of the grievance**, including the strength of the evidence, the applicable provisions of the collective agreement, and the likely outcome at arbitration based on comparable cases; 2. **The interests of the grievor**, including the seriousness of the discipline, the impact on the grievor and their family, and the grievor's length of service and work record; 3. **The interests of the bargaining unit as a whole**, including the need to ensure that the collective agreement is enforced, the need to challenge unjust discipline, and the need to allocate the union's limited resources (including time, money, and access to legal counsel) in a way that serves the membership as a whole; and 4. **The cost of arbitration**, including legal fees, the arbitrator's fees, and the cost of preparing and presenting the case, which in a discharge case typically ranges from $15,000 to $25,000 or more. The union does not make decisions about arbitration based solely on cost. However, the union has a duty to all of its members to use its resources responsibly. Where a grievance has poor prospects of success, the union must consider whether the cost of arbitration is justified, or whether those resources would be better used to represent other members whose grievances have stronger merit. In your case, the union has weighed your significant interest in having your grievance heard against the assessment that the grievance has poor prospects of success. The grievance committee discussed your case at length on March 10, 2026, and considered your arguments and your personal circumstances. The committee concluded, based on the legal opinion and the review of the file, that the cost of arbitration was not justified given the likely outcome. This was not an easy decision, and it was not made lightly. The union recognizes that you have lost your employment and your income, and that this has serious consequences for you. However, the union has concluded that referring the grievance to arbitration would not likely result in your reinstatement, and that the union's resources are better directed to cases with stronger prospects of success. ## Concerns You Have Raised In your communications with the union, you have raised a number of concerns about the way your grievance was handled. I want to address those concerns directly. **Emails in March 2026** You have stated that you sent two emails to the union in March 2026 and that the union ignored them. The union's records show that you sent emails on March 3, 2026 and March 6, 2026. The March 3 email was responded to on March 8, 2026 (this was the third written update provided to you). The March 6 email was received on a Friday afternoon; the union representative was away from the office on March 7 and 8 (a weekend), and responded on March 9, 2026. The union acknowledges that the response to the March 6 email took three days, and that this may have felt like a long time given the stress you were under. However, the union did respond to both emails, and the delay in responding to the second email was brief and due to the representative's schedule. The union did not ignore your emails. **The Decision Was Made to Save Money** You have stated that the union decided not to advance your grievance in order to save money. As I have explained above, the union's decision was based on the merits of the grievance and the assessment that it had poor prospects of success at arbitration. The cost of arbitration was one factor the union considered, but it was not the only factor, and it was not the determining factor. The union advances grievances to arbitration regularly, including discharge grievances, where the union assesses that the grievance has reasonable prospects of success. In your case, the legal opinion and the grievance committee's review led to the conclusion that the prospects were poor, and that conclusion drove the decision. **The Steward's Comment** You have stated that a steward called you "a lost cause" in front of coworkers. The union has investigated this allegation. The steward in question has provided a written statement indicating that during a conversation with you and two other members in the lunchroom in mid-March 2026, he said words to the effect of "it's a tough case" or "it's an uphill battle," and that he does not recall using the phrase "a lost cause." The other member present has not responded to the union's request for a statement. The union takes seriously any allegation that a steward has treated a member disrespectfully. Based on the investigation, the union cannot confirm that the steward used the specific phrase you have attributed to him. The union has spoken to the steward about this, and he has acknowledged that the conversation should have been handled differently. The union apologizes for any embarrassment or distress this caused you. However, the steward's comment, even if it was made as you describe, did not affect the union's decision about your grievance. The decision was made by the grievance committee based on the legal opinion and the review of the file, and the steward who made the comment was not the deciding voice on the committee. ## Your Options You have the right to appeal the union's decision not to advance your grievance to arbitration.

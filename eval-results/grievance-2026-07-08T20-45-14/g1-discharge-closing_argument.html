Nature of grievance: Discharge dated 26 June 2026, for alleged falsification of a lockout verification record on a single occasion

Preliminary Note on Framework

This is a discharge grievance. The analysis proceeds under the established Wm. Scott framework: first, whether the employer had just and reasonable cause for some discipline; second, if so, whether discharge was an excessive response in all the circumstances; and third, if discharge was excessive, what alternative measure is just and equitable. The just cause standard is anchored in Article 5.02 of the 2023 to 2026 Collective Agreement. The onus rests on the employer throughout to establish just cause on clear and cogent evidence, the burden being heightened here because the allegation is one of dishonesty. See Wm. Scott and Company Ltd., [1977] 1 Can LRBR 1. [AUTHORITY: insert Ontario appellate or arbitral authority on the heightened standard of proof for allegations of dishonesty]

Issue 1: Just Cause Challenge

The proposition

The employer has not discharged its onus of proving, on clear and cogent evidence, that the grievor falsified a lockout verification record. Absent proof of a deliberate and dishonest act, there is no just cause for any discipline, and certainly none for discharge.

Anchor

Article 5.02 (just cause). The employer must prove both that the conduct occurred and that it was culpable. Falsification requires proof of intent to deceive, not mere error, omission, or inadvertence.

Supporting facts

  1. The employer alleges a single instance of falsification on 24 June 2026. There is no allegation of a pattern. [EVIDENCE: what the record actually showed and how it was completed]
  2. No investigation was conducted before discipline was imposed, so the employer had no factual foundation on which to distinguish falsification from error. [EVIDENCE: confirmation through Devon Price that no investigation meeting occurred]
  3. The grievor will testify to what she did and why in relation to the verification record. [EVIDENCE: Marta Kovacs on the incident and her state of mind]
  4. Alia Rahman observed conditions on the floor before the incident that bear on what occurred. [EVIDENCE: Alia Rahman on floor conditions and sequence of events]

Anticipated employer position and the union's answer

The employer will characterize the completion of the record as deliberate falsification of safety documentation and will argue that the safety context is aggravating. The union answers that the safety context raises, rather than lowers, the standard of proof the employer must meet, and that the employer cannot prove intent to deceive when it never investigated, never interviewed the grievor, and never tested the innocent explanations available. A charge of falsification collapses if the conduct is equally consistent with error or omission. [EVIDENCE: any admission by the supervisor that the grievor was not asked for an explanation]

Issue 2: Grievance Time Limits

The proposition

The grievance is timely, or in the alternative the arbitrator should exercise any available authority to relieve against a technical timeliness objection so that the merits are heard.

Anchor

Article 11 (grievance procedure). The filing deadline is ten working days, expiring 8 July 2026. The time limits are stated to be mandatory.

Supporting facts

  1. The discharge letter is dated 26 June 2026. [EVIDENCE: date of receipt of the discharge letter by the grievor]
  2. The grievance must be filed by 8 July 2026. [EVIDENCE: date the grievance was in fact filed]

Anticipated employer position and the union's answer

Because the language is mandatory, the employer may raise timeliness if the grievance was filed even one day late. The union's answer depends on the filing date. If the grievance was filed within the ten working day window, the objection does not arise. If any question of computation is raised, the union will address the proper counting of working days and will rely on section 48(16) of the Labour Relations Act, 1995, which empowers the arbitrator to extend time limits where there are reasonable grounds and the opposing party is not substantially prejudiced. [EVIDENCE: exact filing date and any explanation for computation]

Issue 3: Weber Exclusive Forum

The proposition

This dispute, in its essential character, arises from the interpretation, application, or administration of the collective agreement and falls within the exclusive jurisdiction of this arbitration. There is no jurisdictional bar to the arbitrator deciding every dimension of the grievance, including any human rights or statutory dimension raised on the facts.

Anchor

Weber v. Ontario Hydro, [1995] 2 SCR 929. The essential character of the dispute, a discharge under a collective agreement, is squarely arbitral. The arbitrator's remedial authority extends to Employment Standards and Human Rights Code matters arising in the workplace pursuant to Parry Sound (District) Social Services Administration Board v. O.P.S.E.U., Local 324, 2003 SCC 42.

Supporting facts

  1. The grievance concerns discharge under Article 5.02 of the collective agreement. [EVIDENCE: the pleaded grievance and its characterization]

Anticipated employer position and the union's answer

The employer is unlikely to contest arbitral jurisdiction over a discharge grievance. To the extent the employer suggests any element belongs in another forum, the union answers that the essential character of the entire dispute is the discipline and its consequences, which is exclusively arbitral.

Issue 4: Duty of Fair Representation Exposure

The proposition

The union must advance this grievance to its conclusion in a manner that is not arbitrary, discriminatory, or in bad faith. Given the strength of the merits and the procedural defects in the employer's process, careful and diligent carriage is required and is being provided.

Anchor

Section 74 of the Labour Relations Act, 1995. This is an internal risk-management note for the file and is not to be argued to the arbitrator.

Supporting facts and file-management points

  1. The grievance has not yet been filed and the mandatory filing deadline is 8 July 2026. The single most important step to protect both the grievor and the union is to file within time. [EVIDENCE: confirmation of timely filing]
  2. The merits are strong: an 18 year employee, no investigation, no union representation at the meeting, and a four minute discharge meeting. Diligent carriage is plainly warranted.
  3. All witness statements and the discharge documentation should be preserved and carriage decisions documented in the file.

Issue 5: Prior Record Challenge

The proposition

The grievor's disciplinary record is effectively clean and cannot support discharge or the aggravation of penalty. The sole prior entry has passed its sunset date and must be disregarded.

Anchor

The sunset clause in the collective agreement provides a 24 month currency period for discipline. Article 5.02 and the general principle that discipline is progressive and remedial.

Supporting facts

  1. The only prior discipline is a written warning in 2019 for late reporting, which was not grieved. [EVIDENCE: the 2019 written warning and its date]
  2. The 24 month sunset period expired long before the 2026 incident, so the 2019 warning is spent and inactive. [EVIDENCE: application of the sunset clause to the 2019 date]
  3. The grievor therefore stands before the arbitrator with, in substance, an unblemished record over 18 years of service. [EVIDENCE: absence of any other discipline]

Anticipated employer position and the union's answer

The employer may seek to reference the 2019 warning as background. The union answers that a warning outside the sunset period cannot be relied upon to justify or aggravate discipline, and that an unrelated late reporting matter from 2019 has no probative connection to an allegation of falsification in 2026 in any event.

Issue 6: Procedural Defects

The proposition

The employer's process was fundamentally deficient. There was no investigation before discipline, no union representation at the disciplinary meeting, and the meeting lasted only four minutes. These defects are not mere technicalities; they demonstrate that the decision to discharge was made without a fair and adequate factual foundation.

Anchor

Article 5.02 and the requirement that discipline be for just cause, which imports a fair process. Any specific provision governing representation at disciplinary meetings should be inserted. [AUTHORITY: insert collective agreement article on union representation at disciplinary meetings if one exists]

Supporting facts

  1. No investigation was conducted before the discipline was imposed. The employer reached a conclusion of falsification without gathering or testing the facts. [EVIDENCE: Marta Kovacs and Devon Price confirming that no investigative interview occurred]
  2. There was no union representation at the disciplinary meeting. [EVIDENCE: Devon Price on his exclusion from or absence at the meeting]
  3. The discharge meeting lasted approximately four minutes, which confirms that no meaningful opportunity to respond was given before the decision was carried out. [EVIDENCE: Devon Price and Marta Kovacs on the length and content of the meeting, including what the supervisor said]

Anticipated employer position and the union's answer

The employer may argue that procedural defects do not void an otherwise justified discharge. The union answers on two levels. First, where the employer never investigated, there is no reliable factual basis on which just cause could ever have been assessed, so the defect goes to the merits and not merely to process. Second, even if some cause were found, the absence of investigation, the denial of representation, and the perfunctory four minute meeting are significant mitigating and procedural considerations that weigh heavily against discharge. [AUTHORITY: insert arbitral authority on the effect of failure to investigate and denial of representation on penalty]

Mitigating Factors

The mitigating factors are advanced not as a list but as a connected picture of why discharge was excessive even if some misconduct were found.

Long and unblemished service. The grievor has approximately 18 years of seniority from 17 March 2008 in a skilled millwright classification. Long service in a skilled trade is a substantial mitigating factor and weighs strongly toward a remedial rather than terminal response. [EVIDENCE: confirmation of continuous service and classification]

Effectively clean record. The only prior discipline is a spent 2019 written warning outside the sunset period. There is no disciplinary history that could justify moving directly to discharge. [EVIDENCE: the disciplinary record in full]

Failure to investigate. The employer imposed the most serious penalty available without conducting any investigation. This deprived the grievor of the chance to explain and deprived the employer of the facts. It undermines any submission that discharge was a measured response. [EVIDENCE: absence of any investigation]

Denial of union representation. The grievor faced a discharge meeting without union representation. This procedural failure aggravates the unfairness of the process and supports substitution of a lesser penalty. [EVIDENCE: Devon Price on representation]

A single alleged incident. The allegation concerns one occasion only, with no suggestion of repetition. A single incident by a long service employee with a clean record is the paradigm case for a corrective rather than a terminal response. [EVIDENCE: confirmation there is one alleged incident only]

Issue 7: Reinstatement and Make-Whole Remedy

The proposition

The discharge should be set aside in its entirety and the grievor reinstated and made whole. In the alternative, if any cause is found, a lesser penalty should be substituted.

Anchor

Article 5.02 and the arbitrator's remedial jurisdiction under section 48 of the Labour Relations Act, 1995, including the authority to substitute a just and equitable penalty for discharge.

Order sought

  1. A declaration that the employer did not have just cause to discharge the grievor.
  2. An order rescinding the discharge and reinstating the grievor to her position as Millwright in the Maintenance Department with full seniority as though the discharge had not occurred.
  3. An order for full compensation for lost wages and benefits from the date of discharge to the date of reinstatement, estimated at approximately $96,000 in back pay subject to proof, together with interest and adjustment for any statutory or deductible amounts. [EVIDENCE: precise calculation of lost earnings and benefits and any mitigation earnings]
  4. An order removing the discharge and all related documentation from the grievor's record.
  5. In the alternative, if some misconduct is found, substitution of a lesser penalty that is just and equitable, having regard to the grievor's 18 years of service, her effectively clean record, and the procedural defects described above, with corresponding compensation for the period of any excess penalty.
  6. An order that the arbitrator remain seized with respect to the implementation and calculation of the remedy.

Anticipated employer position and the union's answer

The employer may argue that the trust relationship is irreparably damaged and that reinstatement is inappropriate for a falsification of safety documentation. The union answers that reinstatement is the normal remedy for an unjust discharge, that no proper factual foundation for a finding of falsification exists on this record, and that where the employer never investigated and never afforded representation it cannot credibly assert that the employment relationship is beyond repair. [AUTHORITY: insert authority on reinstatement as the presumptive remedy and on when compensation in lieu is exceptional]

Conclusion

The employer bears the onus and has not met it. It alleges a single act of falsification without having investigated, without having offered union representation, and after a four minute meeting, against an employee with 18 years of service and no live disciplinary record. On this record there is no just cause for any discipline, and discharge is in any event an excessive and disproportionate response. The union asks that the grievance be allowed, that the grievor be reinstated, and that she be made whole.

Union's Arbitration Brief

In the Matter of an Arbitration Between IAM Local 235 ("the Union") and Trillium Paper Products Inc ("the Employer") respecting the discharge of Marta Kovacs ("the Grievor").

Prepared by: Jordan Whitfield, Whitfield Labour Law, counsel for the Union.

Note: This is a DRAFT prepared for review by union counsel and the responsible labour relations officer before use.

1. Overview

The Grievor, Marta Kovacs, is a Millwright in the Maintenance department at the Employer's Cornwall mill with approximately eighteen years of continuous service dating to 17 March 2008. On 24 June 2026, the Employer discharged the Grievor on the basis of an allegation that she falsified a lockout verification record on a single occasion. The Employer imposed the most serious penalty available, discharge, without conducting any investigation and without affording the Grievor union representation at a disciplinary meeting that lasted only four minutes. The Union submits that the Employer did not have just cause for discharge, that the discipline is procedurally and substantively defective, and that discharge is in any event grossly disproportionate to the alleged conduct of a long-service employee with a nearly unblemished record. The Union seeks reinstatement with full back pay, benefits, and seniority, and, in the alternative, substitution of a lesser penalty.

2. The Parties and the Collective Agreement

The Union is the exclusive bargaining agent for the bargaining unit that includes the Maintenance classification at the Employer's Cornwall mill. The parties are bound by the 2023 to 2026 Collective Agreement.

The grievance and arbitration procedure is set out at Article 11 of the Collective Agreement. The time limits in Article 11 are expressed as mandatory. The just cause provision is Article 5.02. The Union will rely on the text of Article 5.02 as reproduced at the hearing; where the intake material does not set out the verbatim wording of Article 5.02, the Union reserves the right to quote the article in full once the Collective Agreement is entered as an exhibit.

Article 5.02 requires that the Employer establish just cause before imposing discipline or discharge. It is a foundational protection: the burden rests on the Employer to prove, on a balance of probabilities, both the misconduct alleged and that discharge is the appropriate response.

3. Statement of Facts

  1. The Grievor, Marta Kovacs, is employed as a Millwright in the Maintenance department at the Employer's Cornwall mill.
  2. The Grievor's seniority date is 17 March 2008. As of the date of discharge she had approximately eighteen years of continuous service.
  3. The Grievor's disciplinary record consists of a single written warning issued in 2019 for late reporting. That warning was not grieved.
  4. The Collective Agreement contains a sunset clause providing that discipline is removed from the record after 24 months.
  5. The 2019 written warning is therefore well beyond the 24-month sunset period and, on the Union's position, forms no part of the Grievor's active disciplinary record.
  6. On 24 June 2026, the Employer alleged that the Grievor falsified a lockout verification record on one occasion.
  7. The Employer conducted no investigation into the alleged incident before imposing discipline.
  8. The Grievor was not afforded union representation at the disciplinary meeting.
  9. The disciplinary meeting lasted approximately four minutes.
  10. The Employer discharged the Grievor by letter dated 26 June 2026. The Employer's stated ground for discharge was "falsification of safety documentation."
  11. Steward Devon Price is expected to give evidence regarding the disciplinary meeting and what the supervisor said during it.
  12. Coworker Alia Rahman is expected to give evidence regarding what she observed on the floor before the alleged incident.
  13. The Grievor is expected to give evidence regarding the incident, the meeting, and her service record.

4. Issues

  1. Did the Employer have just cause to discharge the Grievor?
  2. If not, what is the appropriate remedy?
  3. Do the procedural defects (no investigation and no union representation) undermine the discipline?
  4. Does the 2019 written warning form any part of the active disciplinary record, given the 24-month sunset clause?
  5. If some misconduct is established, is discharge excessive, and should a lesser penalty be substituted?

5. Argument

A. The Employer Bears the Burden Under the William Scott Framework

The governing analytical framework for discharge and discipline grievances requires the arbitrator to consider three questions: first, whether the employee gave the employer just cause for some form of discipline; second, if so, whether the decision to discharge was an excessive response in all the circumstances; and third, if so, what alternative penalty should be substituted as just and equitable. This framework, articulated in Re United Steelworkers of America and William Scott & Co., [1977] 1 Can LRBR 1 (B.C.L.R.B.), is applied throughout Ontario arbitral jurisprudence.

The Union submits that the Employer cannot satisfy the first branch. The Employer alleges a single instance of falsification but conducted no investigation whatsoever to establish that the record was in fact false, that the Grievor was responsible, or that any inaccuracy was deliberate as opposed to inadvertent. Falsification, as a form of misconduct going to honesty, requires proof of intent to deceive. An honest error, a misunderstanding, or a clerical inaccuracy is not falsification. Absent any investigation, the Employer has no evidentiary basis to establish the intentional dishonesty its own characterisation ("falsification") requires.

B. Procedural Defects

The manner in which this discharge was imposed is fundamentally unfair. Two defects are decisive.

  1. No investigation. The Employer imposed the capital penalty of employment on an eighteen-year employee without conducting any investigation. Arbitrators consistently hold that an employer must conduct a fair and adequate investigation before imposing serious discipline, including affording the employee an opportunity to respond to the allegations. A discharge imposed without any investigation cannot stand as a fair or reliable determination of misconduct.
  2. No union representation. The Grievor was denied union representation at the disciplinary meeting. The four-minute duration of the meeting confirms that it was not a genuine opportunity to be heard but a pre-determined delivery of a decision already made. This deprived the Grievor of a meaningful chance to explain the circumstances and deprived the Union of its representational role.

These defects go both to the reliability of the Employer's conclusions and to the fairness of the process. They weigh heavily against any finding of just cause and, at minimum, support substitution of a lesser penalty and a full make-whole remedy.

C. The Prior Record Challenge and the Sunset Clause

The Grievor's only prior discipline is a 2019 written warning for late reporting. The Collective Agreement contains a 24-month sunset clause. By operation of that clause, the 2019 warning was removed from the Grievor's record years before the events in issue. It cannot be relied upon by the Employer for any purpose, and the Grievor must be treated as an employee with an effectively clean record over eighteen years of service. Long, clean service is a powerful mitigating factor and, in the Union's submission, is dispositive on the question of penalty.

D. Proportionality: Discharge Is Excessive

Even if the Employer could establish some culpable conduct, discharge is grossly disproportionate. The relevant mitigating factors are compelling: approximately eighteen years of service; a record with no active discipline; a single alleged incident; and the absence of any investigation or fair process. Arbitrators routinely substitute lesser penalties where a long-service employee with a clean record commits an isolated act, particularly where the employer's process was deficient. The Union submits that discharge for a single alleged documentation error, imposed after a four-minute meeting with no investigation and no representation, is precisely the sort of excessive response that the second branch of William Scott is designed to correct.

E. Time Limits and Preservation of the Grievance

The Union is alive to the mandatory time limits in Article 11. The grievance has not yet been filed. The filing deadline is ten working days under Article 11, expiring on 8 July 2026. The Union will ensure the grievance is filed within the mandatory limit to preserve arbitrability. Counsel should confirm the filing date on file before the hearing so that no preliminary objection on timeliness can succeed.

F. Exclusive Forum

The dispute arises expressly and inferentially out of the Collective Agreement (a discharge alleged to be without just cause under Article 5.02). Pursuant to the principles in Weber v. Ontario Hydro, [1995] 2 S.C.R. 929, the arbitrator has exclusive jurisdiction over the essential character of this dispute. This is the correct and only forum for the Grievor's claim.

G. Duty of Fair Representation Considerations

The Union notes internally, and for the benefit of the responsible labour relations officer, that the grievance has not yet been filed and that the mandatory ten-working-day limit expires on 8 July 2026. To avoid any exposure under the Union's duty of fair representation, filing must occur within that period and diligent carriage must be documented throughout. This paragraph is included for internal review and is not intended to form part of the argument advanced against the Employer.

6. Remedy

The presumptive remedy for a discharge found to be without just cause is reinstatement with a make-whole order. The Union seeks:

  1. An order quashing the discharge and reinstating the Grievor to her position as Millwright without loss of seniority (seniority to run continuously from 17 March 2008);
  2. Full back pay from the date of discharge to the date of reinstatement, estimated at approximately $96,000, subject to adjustment on the evidence;
  3. Restoration of all benefits, pension contributions and credited service, and all other entitlements the Grievor would have earned but for the discharge;
  4. Interest on all monetary amounts;
  5. Removal of the discharge and all related documentation from the Grievor's file.

In the alternative, if any culpable conduct is established, the Union seeks substitution of a lesser penalty proportionate to the conduct and to the Grievor's long, clean service, together with a corresponding make-whole order for the balance.

7. Order Requested

  1. A declaration that the Employer did not have just cause to discharge the Grievor;
  2. A declaration that the disciplinary process was procedurally defective in that no investigation was conducted and the Grievor was denied union representation;
  3. A declaration that the 2019 written warning, by operation of the 24-month sunset clause, forms no part of the Grievor's active record;
  4. An order reinstating the Grievor with full back pay (estimated at $96,000), benefits, pension, and seniority, with interest;
  5. In the alternative, substitution of a lesser penalty with a corresponding make-whole remedy;
  6. An order that the arbitrator remain seized with respect to the implementation and quantification of the remedy.

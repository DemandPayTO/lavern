# DRAFT FOR REVIEW **RE: Grievance #2026-419-008 – Discharge – Marek Kowalski** Dear Marek Kowalski: This letter provides an update on your grievance concerning your discharge on February 3, 2026. ## Current Status Your grievance was filed on February 6, 2026, and advanced to Step 2 of the grievance procedure under Article 7 of the collective agreement. Bayfront Distribution Centres Inc denied the grievance at Step 2 on March 12, 2026, maintaining that your discharge was for just cause based on the positive post-incident drug test and the employer's zero-tolerance policy. Following receipt of the Step 2 denial, the union obtained a legal opinion from external labour counsel to assess the merits of proceeding to arbitration. That opinion identified significant evidentiary and legal challenges, including the employer's reliance on the positive test result, the application of the zero-tolerance policy, and the prior disciplinary record. Based on that assessment, the union's bargaining unit committee reviewed the file and determined that referral to arbitration was not in the best interests of the membership or likely to succeed. The union provided you with written updates on February 20, March 5, March 18, and April 2, 2026, explaining the steps being taken and the reasons for the decision not to arbitrate. ## What Happens Next The union has been served with a duty of fair representation application under section 74 of the Labour Relations Act, 1995, filed by you with the Ontario Labour Relations Board. The application alleges that the union acted arbitrarily, discriminatorily, or in bad faith in its handling of your grievance. The union will be responding to that application. The response will address the allegations in the application, including the claims that the union ignored your emails in March 2026, that the decision was made to save money, and that a steward made inappropriate comments about your case in front of coworkers. The union's position is that it fulfilled its duty of fair representation by investigating your grievance, obtaining a legal opinion, involving the bargaining unit committee in the decision, and providing you with regular written updates throughout the process. The Board will schedule a hearing date once all materials have been filed. That hearing will determine whether the union breached its duty of fair representation. If the Board finds no breach, your application will be dismissed. If the Board finds a breach, it may order the union to take further steps, which could include referring your grievance to arbitration. ## What the Union Is Doing The union is preparing a written response to your section 74 application. That response will include the four written updates sent to you, the minutes of the bargaining unit committee meeting at which your grievance was discussed, the legal opinion obtained from external counsel, and affidavit evidence from the union representatives involved in your case. The union is also reviewing the specific allegations in your application, including the claim about ignored emails and the alleged comment by a steward, and will provide a full factual response to each. The union's legal counsel, Whitfield Labour Law, is handling the Board proceeding on behalf of Teamsters Local 419. ## What We Need From You Nothing is required from you at this time. If the Board schedules a hearing or requests additional information, we will contact you immediately. Please ensure that the union has your current mailing address, email address, and telephone number. If any of your contact information has changed since February 2026, please advise us as soon as possible. ## Questions If you have any questions about this update or the section 74 process, please contact Jordan Whitfield at Whitfield Labour Law.

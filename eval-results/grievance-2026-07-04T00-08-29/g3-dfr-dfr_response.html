Response of the Union to Application under Section 74 of the Labour Relations Act, 1995

Applicant (Complainant): Marek Kowalski, Warehouse Associate

Responding Party (Union): Teamsters Local 419

Employer: Bayfront Distribution Centres Inc

Prepared by: Jordan Whitfield, Whitfield Labour Law

Note: This is a DRAFT prepared for review by the responsible labour relations officer and union counsel before filing.

1. Overview

The Union's decision not to refer the Applicant's discharge grievance to arbitration was a considered and honest exercise of representational judgment, made after a legal opinion assessed the prospects of success as poor and after review by a bargaining-unit committee. The decision was not arbitrary, discriminatory, or made in bad faith, and the application discloses no breach of the standard in section 74 of the Labour Relations Act, 1995.

2. Background

The Union is the certified bargaining agent for a bargaining unit that includes warehouse associates employed by the Employer. The parties are governed by a collective agreement whose grievance procedure appears at Article 7.

The Applicant was employed as a Warehouse Associate with a seniority date of January 10, 2022. On February 3, 2026, the Applicant was discharged following a positive post-incident drug test administered after a forklift collision. The Employer's stated ground for discharge was impairment at work contrary to a zero-tolerance policy.

The Applicant's prior disciplinary record included a verbal warning in 2024 relating to safety and a written warning in 2025 relating to a near-miss.

The Union filed a grievance on February 6, 2026 (grievance #2026-419-008) challenging the discharge. The grievance was advanced through the grievance procedure to Step 2, which is the final step. The Employer delivered its Step 2 response on March 12, 2026 and maintained the discharge.

3. The Union's Process

The Union's handling of the grievance was diligent and documented throughout. The following materials evidence the Union's process and are available to the Board and to the Applicant:

  1. The grievance filed on February 6, 2026 and advanced through Article 7 to Step 2 (final).
  2. A legal opinion obtained by the Union that assessed the prospects of success on the merits, including the arbitral treatment of discharges arising from positive post-incident drug tests under zero-tolerance policies, and that concluded the prospects of overturning the discharge at arbitration were poor.
  3. Minutes of a bargaining-unit committee review, at which the committee considered the merits assessment, the applicable arbitral jurisprudence, the Applicant's prior disciplinary record, the cost and likelihood of success at arbitration, and the interests of the bargaining unit as a whole, before deciding to decline referral to arbitration.
  4. Four written updates provided to the Applicant over the course of the grievance, documenting the Union's steps and communications.

In assessing the merits, the Union weighed the strength of the Employer's evidence of a positive post-incident test following a collision, the existence of a zero-tolerance policy, the Applicant's prior record, and the reasonable likelihood of success at arbitration relative to the cost of proceeding. The Union honestly concluded, on the strength of a legal opinion and committee deliberation, that the grievance did not warrant referral to arbitration. That is precisely the kind of substantive judgment the duty of fair representation protects.

4. The Law

Section 74 of the Labour Relations Act, 1995 prohibits a union from acting in a manner that is arbitrary, discriminatory, or in bad faith in its representation of employees in the bargaining unit. The section does not require a union to advance every grievance to arbitration, and it does not entitle a member to insist on arbitration where the union has, on a considered basis, concluded the grievance lacks sufficient merit.

The following principles apply:

  1. The union is entitled to weigh the merits of a grievance, the arbitral jurisprudence, the cost of proceeding, the likelihood of success, and the interests of the bargaining unit as a whole.
  2. The Board does not sit in appeal of the union's judgment on the merits. The question is whether the decision-making process was honest, considered, and undertaken with genuine attention to the member's interests, not whether the union was correct.
  3. Mere negligence, error in judgment, or a decision that later proves mistaken does not breach the duty. The standard is directed at the manner in which the union acted.
  4. The onus rests on the complainant to establish a breach, and to state a prima facie case in the application itself.

5. Response to the Specific Allegations

  1. Allegation that the Union "did nothing." The Union denies this allegation. The Union filed the grievance promptly on February 6, 2026, advanced it through Article 7 to the final step, obtained a legal opinion on the merits, convened a bargaining-unit committee to review the file, and provided the Applicant with four written updates. The record demonstrates active and diligent representation, not inaction.
  2. Allegation that the grievance was "settled cheap." The Union denies this characterization. The grievance was not settled; the Union declined to refer it to arbitration following a merits assessment concluding the prospects of success were poor. A decision not to arbitrate a grievance with poor prospects is a legitimate exercise of judgment and is not a settlement.
  3. Allegation that the Union ignored two emails from the Applicant in March 2026. The Union denies that it disregarded the Applicant's communications. The Union provided four written updates to the Applicant over the course of the file. [REVIEW: PROCESS GAP] The reviewer should confirm from the file whether the two March 2026 emails identified by the Applicant received a documented response, and, if so, produce that correspondence; if a response is not on file, counsel should assess the timing and content of the March communications before this paragraph is finalized. In any event, an isolated delay or omission in correspondence does not, without more, meet the arbitrary, discriminatory, or bad-faith standard where the substantive decision was considered and honest.
  4. Allegation that the decision was made "to save money." The Union denies that cost was the operative or improper basis for the decision. Cost and likelihood of success are legitimate factors a union is entitled to weigh, together with the merits, the arbitral jurisprudence, the Applicant's prior record, and the interests of the bargaining unit. The decision here rested on a legal opinion assessing poor prospects on the merits, not on cost alone.
  5. Allegation that a steward called the Applicant "a lost cause" in front of coworkers. [REVIEW: PROCESS GAP] The reviewer should investigate this allegation with the steward before this paragraph is finalized. Subject to that investigation, the Union's position is that the alleged remark, even if made, is not shown to have influenced the decision-making process, which was carried out through a legal opinion and a bargaining-unit committee review. An intemperate comment by an individual, without a connection to the impugned decision, does not establish that the Union acted arbitrarily, in a discriminatory manner, or in bad faith. The Union will address the specifics once the facts are confirmed.

6. Relief Requested

The Union respectfully requests that the Board:

  1. Dismiss the application on the basis that it discloses no prima facie breach of section 74, without an oral hearing where the Board considers it appropriate to do so; and
  2. Grant such further relief as the Board considers just.

The Union acknowledges the difficulty of the Applicant's circumstances and does not dispute the sincerity of the Applicant's disappointment. Its position is confined to the legal standard: the decision was a considered, honest, and reasoned exercise of judgment that falls within the protection of section 74.

All of which is respectfully submitted as a DRAFT for review.

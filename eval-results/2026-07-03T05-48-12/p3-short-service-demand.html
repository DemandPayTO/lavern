Demand Letter re: Wrongful Dismissal of Devon Tran

Dear Sir/Madam:

RE: Wrongful Dismissal of Devon Tran — Without Prejudice

We are employment counsel with Whitfield Employment Law and have been retained by Devon Tran (our "client") in connection with the termination of their employment by Orchard Lane Bistro Ltd (the "Company"). The purpose of this letter is to set out our client's entitlements arising from that termination and to seek an amicable resolution of this matter without the need for litigation.

Our client commenced employment with the Company on November 4, 2024, in the position of Assistant Manager, at a base salary of $52,000 per year. Our client's employment continued for approximately one year and seven months until it was terminated on June 12, 2026.

On June 12, 2026, the Company terminated our client's employment. On the basis of the information available to us, our client's dismissal was without cause and was not accompanied by adequate notice or pay in lieu thereof. Accordingly, our client is entitled to both statutory and common law damages, as set out below.

1. Wrongful Dismissal — Common Law Reasonable Notice

At common law, our client is entitled to reasonable notice of the termination of their employment, or pay in lieu thereof. In the absence of an enforceable termination provision limiting our client's entitlements, reasonable notice is determined by the well-established factors set out in Bardal v Globe & Mail, [1960] OJ No 149, namely the character of the employment, the length of service, the age of the employee, and the availability of similar employment having regard to the experience, training, and qualifications of the employee.

Applying the Bardal factors to our client's circumstances — including their position as Assistant Manager, their age of 29 at the time of termination, and their period of service — it is our position that our client is entitled to a common law reasonable notice period in the range of two to three months. Should the Company seek to rely on any written termination clause to limit our client's entitlements, we put you on notice that such a clause must comply strictly with the Employment Standards Act, 2000 in all respects. A termination provision that contravenes the Act — including through an unenforceable "for cause" provision — renders the entire termination scheme void and unenforceable, as confirmed by the Court of Appeal in Waksdale v Swegon North America Inc, 2020 ONCA 391, and as reflected in Machtinger v HOJ Industries Ltd, [1992] 1 SCR 986. In that event, our client's entitlement to common law reasonable notice is fully preserved.

2. Statutory Entitlements Under the Employment Standards Act, 2000

Independent of and in addition to the common law analysis, our client is entitled to their minimum statutory entitlements under the Employment Standards Act, 2000 (the "ESA"). Pursuant to section 54 and section 57 of the ESA, having completed more than one year but fewer than three years of service, our client is entitled to one week of notice of termination, or pay in lieu thereof, being $1,000.

With respect to statutory severance pay under section 64 of the ESA, our client does not meet the qualifying threshold of five years of service, and accordingly no statutory severance pay is owing. We note, however, that these statutory minimums are a floor and not a ceiling, and do not displace our client's substantially greater common law entitlement to reasonable notice.

Damages Quantification

On the basis of the foregoing, our client's entitlements are particularised as follows:

  1. ESA notice pay (one week pursuant to section 57): $1,000;
  2. ESA severance pay: $0 (qualifying threshold not met);
  3. Common law reasonable notice (two to three months, inclusive of and not in addition to the ESA minimum): $8,667 to $13,000.

We wish to emphasize that our client has an ongoing obligation to mitigate their damages by seeking comparable alternative employment, and our client intends to comply fully with that obligation.

Demand

This figure reflects a reasonable resolution having regard to the entitlements described above, and we trust that it can be resolved amicably.

Any resolution will be conditional upon the provision of our client's Record of Employment (if not already provided) accurately reflecting the termination, and payment of all outstanding wages, accrued vacation pay, and other statutory entitlements owing.

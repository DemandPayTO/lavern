CLAIM

The Plaintiff claims:

  1. Damages in the amount of $190,000 for wrongful dismissal, representing pay in lieu of common law reasonable notice, including base salary, bonus, and all other compensation and benefits the Plaintiff would have earned during the reasonable notice period;
  2. A declaration that the termination provision in the Plaintiff's employment agreement is void, invalid, and unenforceable;
  3. The Plaintiff's statutory entitlements under the Employment Standards Act, 2000, S.O. 2000, c. 41, including notice pay under s. 57 and severance pay under s. 64, to the extent such amounts have not been paid;
  4. Damages for the loss of the Plaintiff's annual bonus and any other incentive or equity compensation through the reasonable notice period;
  5. Pre-judgment and post-judgment interest pursuant to sections 128 and 129 of the Courts of Justice Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. C.43;
  6. The costs of this action on a substantial indemnity basis, plus applicable HST; and
  7. Such further and other relief as this Honourable Court may deem just.

FACTS

1. The Plaintiff, Marcus Webb (the "Plaintiff"), is an individual who at all material times resided in the Province of Ontario and was employed by the Defendant.

2. The Defendant, Cadence Manufacturing Ltd (the "Defendant" or the "Company"), is an employer carrying on business in the Province of Ontario.

3. The Plaintiff commenced employment with the Defendant on April 11, 2011.

4. At the time of termination, the Plaintiff held the position of Plant Operations Manager, a senior managerial position.

5. The Plaintiff was employed continuously by the Defendant for a period of approximately 15 years and 1 month, from April 11, 2011 to May 15, 2026.

6. At the time of termination, the Plaintiff was 54 years of age.

7. At the time of termination, the Plaintiff's compensation consisted of a base salary of $128,000 per annum and an annual bonus of $15,000.

8. The Plaintiff's annual bonus was an integral part of his overall compensation.

9. The Plaintiff's employment was governed by a written employment agreement (the "Employment Agreement"), which contained a termination provision (the "Termination Clause") that provided as follows:

"The Company may terminate your employment at any time without cause upon providing you with the minimum notice or pay in lieu required by the Employment Standards Act, 2000, and nothing more."

10. On May 15, 2026, the Defendant terminated the Plaintiff's employment. The stated reason for the termination was restructuring.

11. The Defendant purported to rely on the Termination Clause to limit the Plaintiff's entitlements upon termination.

12. The Plaintiff states that the Defendant has failed to provide the Plaintiff with his full statutory entitlements under the Employment Standards Act, 2000 and has failed to provide the Plaintiff with common law reasonable notice of termination.

LEGAL BASIS

Invalidity of the Termination Clause

13. The Plaintiff states that the Termination Clause is void, invalid, and unenforceable, and that the Plaintiff is therefore entitled to common law reasonable notice of termination.

14. The Plaintiff states that the Termination Clause attempts to contract out of, and provides for less than, the minimum standards required by the Employment Standards Act, 2000, contrary to s. 5(1) of that Act.

15. The Plaintiff further states that where any part of a termination provision violates the Employment Standards Act, 2000, the entire termination provision is unenforceable, as held in Waksdale v Swegon North America Inc., 2020 ONCA 391. A termination clause must be read as a whole, and an unlawful provision cannot be severed or saved.

16. Accordingly, the Termination Clause fails to rebut the common law presumption that the Plaintiff is entitled to reasonable notice of termination.

Wrongful Dismissal and Reasonable Notice

17. Because the Termination Clause is unenforceable, the Plaintiff is entitled to common law reasonable notice of termination or pay in lieu thereof.

18. In determining the period of reasonable notice, the Court will consider the factors set out in Bardal v Globe & Mail Ltd. (1960), 24 D.L.R. (2d) 140 (Ont. H.C.), including the character of the employment, the Plaintiff's length of service, the Plaintiff's age, and the availability of similar employment having regard to the Plaintiff's experience, training, and qualifications.

19. Having regard to the Bardal factors — the Plaintiff's senior managerial position as Plant Operations Manager, his approximately 15 years of service, his age of 54 at termination, and the limited availability of comparable employment — the Plaintiff is entitled to a reasonable notice period in the range of 16 to 24 months.

Bonus and Other Compensation During the Notice Period

20. The Plaintiff is entitled to be compensated for all forms of compensation he would have earned during the reasonable notice period, including his annual bonus of $15,000 and any other incentive or equity compensation.

21. The Plaintiff relies on Matthews v Ocean Nutrition Canada Ltd., 2020 SCC 26, and states that damages for wrongful dismissal include all compensation and benefits the Plaintiff would have earned during the reasonable notice period, absent clear and unambiguous contractual language removing such entitlement upon termination. No such language exists in the Employment Agreement.

Statutory Entitlements

22. In the alternative, and in any event, the Plaintiff is entitled to his minimum statutory entitlements under the Employment Standards Act, 2000, including:

  1. Notice of termination or pay in lieu thereof pursuant to s. 57; and
  2. Severance pay pursuant to s. 64.

23. The Plaintiff states that the Defendant has failed to satisfy these statutory obligations.

DAMAGES PARTICULARS

24. The Plaintiff particularises his damages as follows:

  1. ESA notice pay (8 weeks pursuant to s. 57): $19,692;
  2. ESA severance pay (pursuant to s. 64): $37,154;
  3. Common law reasonable notice (16 to 24 months of base salary, bonus, and other compensation): $170,667 to $256,000;
  4. Loss of annual bonus ($15,000 per annum) through the reasonable notice period, included within the common law notice claim above.

25. The Plaintiff's statutory entitlements are subsumed within, and are not in addition to, the common law damages claimed, save to the extent the Court finds the Termination Clause enforceable.

26. The Plaintiff limits the total amount claimed in this action to $190,000, exclusive of interest and costs.

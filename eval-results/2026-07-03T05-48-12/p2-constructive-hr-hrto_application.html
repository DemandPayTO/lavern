3. Protected Ground(s)

The Applicant claims discrimination on the following ground(s) enumerated under s. 5 of the Human Rights Code:

  1. Disability (anxiety disorder)

4. Social Area

Employment — s. 5(1) of the Human Rights Code. The Applicant claims the right to equal treatment with respect to employment without discrimination because of disability, and the corresponding duty of the Respondent to accommodate the Applicant's disability-related needs short of undue hardship.

5. Summary of the Complaint

  1. The Applicant, Aisha Osei, was employed by the Respondent, Brightpath Financial Group Inc, from September 3, 2019 to April 20, 2026 in the position of Senior Client Advisor.
  2. The Applicant's annual compensation consisted of a base salary of $92,000 per year, together with commissions in the amount of approximately $22,000 per year.
  3. During the course of the Applicant's employment, the Applicant developed and was diagnosed with an anxiety disorder, a condition constituting a "disability" within the meaning of s. 10(1) of the Human Rights Code.
  4. The Applicant disclosed the anxiety disorder to the Respondent and, in connection with that disclosure, requested a gradual return-to-work schedule as an accommodation.
  5. Specifically, the Applicant made a written request for a four-day work week for the first month upon returning to work.
  6. The Respondent denied the written accommodation request by email, without any discussion, consultation, or inquiry into the Applicant's disability-related needs.
  7. Approximately two weeks after the Applicant disclosed the anxiety disorder and requested the gradual return-to-work schedule, the Applicant began to experience adverse treatment in the workplace.
  8. The adverse treatment culminated in the constructive dismissal of the Applicant effective April 20, 2026.
  9. This Application is filed within the one-year limitation period prescribed by s. 34(1) of the Human Rights Code. The last incident of discrimination occurred on April 20, 2026, and the limitation period expires on April 20, 2028.

6. How the Respondent Discriminated

  1. The Applicant's anxiety disorder is a disability within the meaning of s. 10(1) of the Human Rights Code.
  2. The Respondent had a procedural and substantive duty to accommodate the Applicant's disability to the point of undue hardship, as required under s. 11 and s. 17 of the Human Rights Code.
  3. The Respondent breached its procedural duty to accommodate by denying the Applicant's written request for a four-day work week by email, without any discussion, without individualized assessment, and without any inquiry into whether accommodation could be provided.
  4. The Respondent breached its substantive duty to accommodate by refusing a reasonable and time-limited accommodation (a four-day week during the first month back) without establishing any undue hardship.
  5. The close temporal connection between the disclosure of the disability and accommodation request, and the onset of adverse treatment approximately two weeks later, establishes a nexus between the protected ground (disability) and the adverse treatment.
  6. The adverse treatment, and the ultimate constructive dismissal on April 20, 2026, were connected to the Applicant's disability and the accommodation request, contrary to s. 5(1) of the Human Rights Code.
  7. The Applicant asserts that disability was a factor in the adverse treatment and dismissal, and that it need not be the only or primary factor to constitute a breach of the Code.

7. Remedy Sought

  1. Monetary compensation for injury to dignity, feelings, and self-respect pursuant to s. 45.2(1) of the Human Rights Code, in an amount to be determined by the Tribunal reflecting the seriousness of the discrimination and its effect on the Applicant.
  2. Compensation for lost wages and benefits arising from the constructive dismissal, including lost base salary of $92,000 per year and commissions of approximately $22,000 per year, together with the value of lost benefits.
  3. Total monetary compensation claimed: $120,000 CAD, allocated between injury to dignity, feelings and self-respect, and lost wages and benefits, as the Tribunal may determine.
  4. Reinstatement to the position of Senior Client Advisor, if appropriate in the circumstances, pursuant to s. 45.2(1) of the Human Rights Code.
  5. Policy changes and training orders requiring the Respondent to develop and implement a disability accommodation policy and to provide human rights and accommodation training to management and human resources personnel.
  6. Public interest remedies pursuant to s. 45.2(1) of the Human Rights Code to promote compliance with the Code and prevent similar discrimination against other employees.

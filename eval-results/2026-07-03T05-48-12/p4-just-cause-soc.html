CLAIM

The Plaintiff claims:

  1. Damages in the amount of $320,000 for wrongful dismissal, representing pay in lieu of common law reasonable notice of the termination of the Plaintiff's employment;
  2. A declaration that the Defendant did not have just cause to terminate the Plaintiff's employment;
  3. The Plaintiff's minimum statutory entitlements to termination pay and severance pay under the Employment Standards Act, 2000, S.O. 2000, c. 41, to the extent not otherwise satisfied by the award of common law damages;
  4. Damages for the value of lost pension/RRSP matching contributions and lost extended health and dental benefits over the reasonable notice period;
  5. Moral (aggravated) damages for bad faith in the manner of dismissal;
  6. Prejudgment and postjudgment interest in accordance with sections 128 and 129 of the Courts of Justice Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. C.43;
  7. The costs of this action on a substantial indemnity basis, together with all applicable HST; and
  8. Such further and other relief as this Honourable Court may deem just.

FACTS

1. The Plaintiff, Helena Kovacs, is an individual who at all material times resided in the Province of Ontario. The Plaintiff was 58 years of age at the time of the termination of the Plaintiff's employment.

2. The Defendant, Stellar Freight Systems Inc, is a corporation carrying on business in the Province of Ontario and was at all material times the Plaintiff's employer.

3. The Plaintiff commenced employment with the Defendant on or about February 19, 2007.

4. At the time of termination, the Plaintiff had been continuously employed by the Defendant for approximately 19 years and 1 month.

5. At the time of termination, the Plaintiff held the position of Director of Dispatch, a senior and responsible position within the Defendant's organization.

6. The Plaintiff's compensation at the time of termination consisted of an annual base salary of $143,000, together with pension/RRSP matching contributions and extended health and dental benefits.

7. Throughout the Plaintiff's approximately 19 years of employment, the Plaintiff performed the Plaintiff's duties faithfully, competently, and diligently.

8. On or about March 30, 2026, the Defendant terminated the Plaintiff's employment.

9. The Defendant alleged that it had just cause to terminate the Plaintiff's employment.

10. The Plaintiff denies that the Defendant had just cause to terminate the Plaintiff's employment. The Plaintiff pleads that no conduct on the Plaintiff's part, whether alleged by the Defendant or otherwise, was of such a nature or degree as to justify termination without notice.

11. The Defendant did not provide the Plaintiff with reasonable notice of the termination of the Plaintiff's employment, or pay in lieu thereof.

12. The Defendant further failed to provide the Plaintiff with the Plaintiff's minimum statutory entitlements to termination pay and severance pay under the Employment Standards Act, 2000.

13. In terminating the Plaintiff's employment, the Defendant advanced an unfounded allegation of just cause. The Plaintiff pleads that the Defendant did so without a reasonable basis and in a manner that was untruthful, misleading, and unduly insensitive, thereby breaching the Defendant's obligation of good faith and fair dealing in the manner of dismissal.

14. As a result of the Defendant's conduct, the Plaintiff has suffered and continues to suffer loss of income, loss of benefits, loss of pension/RRSP matching contributions, and mental distress.

LEGAL BASIS

Absence of Just Cause

15. The Plaintiff pleads that the Defendant has failed to establish just cause for termination. The determination of whether misconduct justifies dismissal without notice requires a proportional and contextual analysis, weighing the nature and seriousness of any alleged misconduct against the circumstances of the employment relationship, as set out in McKinley v. BC Tel, 2001 SCC 38.

16. The Plaintiff pleads that any alleged conduct did not, applying the proportionality standard, give rise to a breakdown of the employment relationship sufficient to warrant summary dismissal. Termination without notice was a disproportionate response in all the circumstances.

Wrongful Dismissal and Reasonable Notice

17. In the absence of just cause, the Plaintiff was entitled at common law to reasonable notice of termination, or pay in lieu thereof. The Defendant's failure to provide such notice constitutes wrongful dismissal.

18. The Plaintiff pleads that the period of reasonable notice must be determined having regard to the factors set out in Bardal v. Globe & Mail Ltd. (1960), 24 D.L.R. (2d) 140 (Ont. H.C.), including the Plaintiff's:

  1. Age at termination (58 years);
  2. Length of service (approximately 19 years and 1 month);
  3. Character of employment (a senior management position as Director of Dispatch); and
  4. The availability of similar employment, having regard to the Plaintiff's experience, training, and qualifications.

19. Having regard to these factors, the Plaintiff pleads that a reasonable notice period of 20 to 24 months is appropriate.

Statutory Entitlements

20. The Plaintiff pleads and relies upon the Employment Standards Act, 2000, S.O. 2000, c. 41. Pursuant to section 57, the Plaintiff is entitled to a minimum of 8 weeks' notice of termination or termination pay in lieu thereof. Pursuant to section 64, having more than five years of service with an employer whose payroll meets the statutory threshold, the Plaintiff is entitled to severance pay calculated in accordance with section 65.

21. The Plaintiff pleads that these statutory entitlements constitute a floor and are not satisfied by, nor do they reduce, the Plaintiff's common law entitlement to reasonable notice save to the extent of amounts actually paid.

Bad Faith in the Manner of Dismissal

22. The Plaintiff pleads that the Defendant breached its duty of good faith and fair dealing in the manner of dismissal, as recognized in Honda Canada Inc. v. Keays, 2008 SCC 39. In particular, the Defendant advanced an unfounded allegation of just cause without reasonable basis, in a manner that was untruthful, misleading, and unduly insensitive.

23. The Plaintiff pleads that this conduct caused the Plaintiff mental distress that was reasonably foreseeable at the time the contract of employment was made, and that the Plaintiff is entitled to moral (aggravated) damages as compensation.

DAMAGES PARTICULARS

The Plaintiff claims damages, particularized as follows:

  1. Statutory termination pay (ESA s. 57): 8 weeks — $22,000;
  2. Statutory severance pay (ESA ss. 64–65): $52,546;
  3. Common law reasonable notice: 20 to 24 months of base salary, in the range of $238,333 to $286,000, inclusive of the value of lost pension/RRSP matching contributions and lost extended health and dental benefits over the notice period;
  4. Moral (aggravated) damages for bad faith in the manner of dismissal, in an amount to be determined at trial;
  5. Total claimed: $320,000.

The Plaintiff pleads that the statutory entitlements set out above are subsumed within, and are not additional to, the total sum claimed, save to the extent that any common law entitlement exceeds the statutory minimum.

CLAIM

The Plaintiff claims:

  1. Damages for wrongful dismissal in the amount of $320,000, representing common law reasonable notice;
  2. A declaration that the Defendant did not have just cause to terminate the Plaintiff's employment;
  3. Payment of the Plaintiff's statutory entitlements pursuant to the Employment Standards Act, 2000, S.O. 2000, c. 41, including notice pay of $22,000 and severance pay of $52,546;
  4. Moral (aggravated) damages arising from the Defendant's bad faith conduct in the manner of dismissal in the amount of $50,000;
  5. Damages for the loss of pension/RRSP matching contributions, and extended health and dental benefits during the notice period;
  6. Prejudgment and postjudgment interest pursuant to sections 128 and 129 of the Courts of Justice Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. C.43;
  7. The costs of this proceeding on a substantial indemnity basis, together with all applicable taxes; and
  8. Such further and other relief as this Honourable Court deems just.

FACTS

The Parties

1. The Plaintiff, Helena Kovacs, is an individual residing in the Province of Ontario. At the time of the termination of his employment, the Plaintiff was 58 years of age.

2. The Defendant, Stellar Freight Systems Inc, is the Plaintiff's former employer and carries on business in the Province of Ontario.

The Employment Relationship

3. The Plaintiff commenced employment with the Defendant on or about February 19, 2007.

4. At the time of his termination, the Plaintiff held the position of Director of Dispatch, a senior and responsible position within the Defendant's organization.

5. The Plaintiff was employed by the Defendant for a continuous period of approximately 19 years and 1 month.

6. At the time of termination, the Plaintiff's compensation consisted of:

  1. A base salary of $143,000 per annum;
  2. Pension/RRSP matching contributions;
  3. Extended health and dental benefits.

7. Throughout his employment, the Plaintiff performed his duties faithfully, competently, and in a manner that met or exceeded the Defendant's reasonable expectations.

The Termination

8. On or about March 30, 2026, the Defendant terminated the Plaintiff's employment.

9. The Defendant alleged that it had just cause to terminate the Plaintiff's employment.

10. The Plaintiff pleads that the Defendant did not have just cause to terminate his employment. The Defendant has failed to identify any misconduct that, viewed in proportion to the surrounding circumstances, would justify the extraordinary remedy of dismissal without notice.

11. In alleging just cause, the Defendant failed to conduct any adequate or fair investigation, failed to afford the Plaintiff any meaningful opportunity to respond, and asserted cause in a manner that was untenable and made in bad faith.

12. The Defendant did not provide the Plaintiff with any notice of termination, pay in lieu of notice, or severance pay upon termination.

Effect of the Termination

13. As a result of the termination, the Plaintiff has suffered a loss of income, benefits, and pension/RRSP matching contributions.

14. Given the Plaintiff's age of 58, his approximately 19 years of service, his senior managerial position as Director of Dispatch, and the character of his employment, the Plaintiff will require a substantial period of time to secure comparable alternate employment.

15. The Plaintiff has taken and continues to take reasonable steps to mitigate his damages by seeking comparable alternate employment.

LEGAL BASIS

Failure to Establish Just Cause

16. The Defendant bears the onus of establishing just cause for dismissal. The Plaintiff pleads that the alleged conduct, even if proven, does not meet the proportionality standard required to justify dismissal for cause, as set out by the Supreme Court of Canada in McKinley v. BC Tel, 2001 SCC 38. The Court must assess whether the nature and degree of any alleged misconduct warranted dismissal, having regard to all of the surrounding circumstances.

17. The Plaintiff pleads that the Defendant cannot discharge its onus and that the termination was therefore without cause.

Wrongful Dismissal and Common Law Reasonable Notice

18. As the Plaintiff was dismissed without just cause and without reasonable notice, he is entitled to damages for wrongful dismissal equal to the compensation he would have earned during a reasonable notice period.

19. The reasonable notice period is to be determined with reference to the factors set out in Bardal v. Globe & Mail Ltd. (1960), 24 D.L.R. (2d) 140 (Ont. H.C.), including the character of the Plaintiff's employment, his length of service, his age, and the availability of similar employment having regard to his experience, training, and qualifications.

20. Having regard to the Bardal factors, the Plaintiff pleads that a reasonable notice period of 20 to 24 months is appropriate in the circumstances.

Statutory Entitlements under the Employment Standards Act, 2000

21. The Plaintiff is entitled to statutory notice of termination pursuant to section 57 of the Employment Standards Act, 2000, S.O. 2000, c. 41 ("ESA"), being 8 weeks of pay in lieu of notice given his length of service.

22. The Plaintiff is further entitled to statutory severance pay pursuant to section 64 of the ESA, having completed more than five years of service with an employer meeting the applicable payroll threshold.

23. The Defendant has failed to pay the Plaintiff any of his statutory entitlements under the ESA.

Bad Faith in the Manner of Dismissal

24. The Plaintiff pleads that the Defendant conducted itself in bad faith in the manner of dismissal, entitling him to moral (aggravated) damages in accordance with Honda Canada Inc. v. Keays, 2008 SCC 39.

25. Particulars of the Defendant's bad faith conduct include:

  1. Alleging just cause without any reasonable or good faith basis for doing so;
  2. Failing to conduct any fair or adequate investigation prior to termination;
  3. Failing to afford the Plaintiff a meaningful opportunity to respond to the allegations against him;
  4. Failing to pay the Plaintiff his minimum statutory entitlements under the ESA; and
  5. Maintaining an untenable assertion of cause in a manner that was unfair and calculated to deprive the Plaintiff of his lawful entitlements.

26. As a result of the Defendant's bad faith conduct, the Plaintiff has suffered mental distress that was a reasonably foreseeable consequence of the manner of dismissal.

DAMAGES PARTICULARS

27. The Plaintiff claims damages particularized as follows:

  1. ESA statutory notice pay (8 weeks): $22,000;
  2. ESA statutory severance pay: $52,546;
  3. Common law reasonable notice (20 to 24 months): $238,333 to $286,000, inclusive of the statutory amounts above;
  4. Loss of pension/RRSP matching contributions and extended health and dental benefits over the notice period, in an amount to be particularized prior to trial;
  5. Moral (aggravated) damages: $50,000;

28. The total amount claimed by the Plaintiff is $320,000, plus interest and costs.

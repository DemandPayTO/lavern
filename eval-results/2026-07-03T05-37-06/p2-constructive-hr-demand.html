Demand Letter — Constructive and Wrongful Dismissal — Aisha Osei and Brightpath Financial Group Inc

Dear Sir/Madam:

Re: Constructive and Wrongful Dismissal of Aisha Osei

We are legal counsel to Aisha Osei (our "client"), who was formerly employed by Brightpath Financial Group Inc as a Senior Client Advisor. We write to advise you of our client's legal position arising from the termination of her employment by way of constructive dismissal, and to set out her entitlements. We trust this matter can be resolved amicably and without the need for litigation.

Employment Background

Our client commenced employment with Brightpath Financial Group Inc on September 3, 2019, in the role of Senior Client Advisor. At the time her employment ended, she was 41 years of age and had accumulated approximately 6 years and 7 months of service. Her compensation consisted of a base salary of $92,000 per year, together with commissions averaging approximately $22,000 per year, for total annual compensation of approximately $114,000.

Termination Facts

Our client took a medical leave of absence in connection with an anxiety disorder. Upon disclosing her diagnosis and requesting a gradual return-to-work schedule, our client was subjected to a series of adverse changes to her employment that began approximately two weeks after her disclosure. Specifically, upon her return she was:

  1. Moved to a junior desk;
  2. Stripped of her established book of clients; and
  3. Subjected to a commission structure that was reduced by approximately one-half.

These changes, taken together, constitute a fundamental and unilateral alteration of the essential terms of her employment. Accordingly, our client treats her employment as having been constructively terminated effective April 20, 2026.

Legal Analysis

1. Constructive Dismissal

The Supreme Court of Canada in Potter v New Brunswick Legal Aid, 2015 SCC 10, established a two-branch test for constructive dismissal. Under the first branch, a constructive dismissal occurs where the employer unilaterally makes a substantial change to an essential term of the employment contract. Under the second branch, a constructive dismissal may be established where the employer's course of conduct, taken as a whole, demonstrates an intention no longer to be bound by the contract.

Our client's case is made out under both branches. The demotion to a junior desk, the removal of her book of clients, and the reduction of her commission structure by approximately half each constitute substantial unilateral changes to essential terms — most notably her compensation and her core duties. Considered cumulatively, they also amount to a course of conduct demonstrating that Brightpath Financial Group Inc no longer intended to be bound by the parties' agreement.

2. Wrongful Dismissal — Common Law Reasonable Notice

Having been constructively dismissed, our client is entitled to reasonable notice of termination at common law, or pay in lieu thereof. The relevant factors were set out in Bardal v Globe & Mail, [1960] OJ No 149, and include the character of the employment, the length of service, the age of the employee, and the availability of similar employment having regard to the employee's experience, training, and qualifications.

Our client was a 41-year-old senior professional with nearly seven years of service in a specialized advisory role. Applying the Bardal factors, we assess her reasonable notice entitlement in the range of 7 to 12 months.

3. ESA Statutory Entitlements

Our client is entitled, at minimum, to her statutory entitlements under the Employment Standards Act, 2000. Pursuant to section 57, she is entitled to statutory notice of termination (or pay in lieu), which we calculate at 6 weeks, or $10,615. Given her length of service and the size of the employer's payroll, she is further entitled to statutory severance pay under section 64, which we calculate at $11,727. These statutory minimums are owing regardless of any common law entitlement and cannot be contracted out of: see Machtinger v HOJ Industries Ltd, [1992] 1 SCR 986.

4. Human Rights Violation — Discrimination

The adverse treatment of our client began approximately two weeks after she disclosed an anxiety disorder — a disability that is a protected ground under the Human Rights Code. The close temporal connection between her disclosure and the adverse changes to her employment strongly supports the inference that her disability was a factor in the treatment she received. Discrimination on the basis of disability entitles our client to damages for injury to dignity, feelings, and self-respect, in addition to her wrongful dismissal entitlements.

5. Failure to Accommodate Disability

Brightpath Financial Group Inc was under a duty to accommodate our client's disability to the point of undue hardship. Our client made a written request for a four-day work week during her first month back from leave. That request was denied by email, without any discussion, exploration of alternatives, or evidence of hardship. This constitutes a clear breach of the employer's procedural and substantive duty to accommodate.

6. Bonus, Commission, and Equity Through the Notice Period

Our client's commissions form an integral part of her compensation. Pursuant to Matthews v Ocean Nutrition Canada Ltd, 2020 SCC 26, our client is entitled to be compensated for all amounts she would have earned, including commissions, throughout the reasonable notice period, as if her employment had continued. The unilateral reduction of her commission structure prior to termination does not diminish this entitlement.

Damages Quantification

On the basis of the foregoing, our client's entitlements include the following:

  1. ESA statutory notice pay (6 weeks): $10,615;
  2. ESA statutory severance pay: $11,727;
  3. Common law reasonable notice (7–12 months), inclusive of base salary and commissions through the notice period pursuant to Matthews: $53,667 to $92,000 (the statutory amounts above being credited against, not additional to, the common law figure);
  4. Damages for discrimination and failure to accommodate under the Human Rights Code (injury to dignity, feelings, and self-respect); and
  5. Continuation of all bonus, commission, and equity entitlements through the notice period.

Demand

In order to resolve this matter without recourse to litigation, our client is prepared to accept the global sum of $120,000 CAD, inclusive of all wrongful dismissal and human rights entitlements, in full and final settlement. This figure reflects a reasonable compromise having regard to the strength of our client's claims across all of the heads of damage set out above.

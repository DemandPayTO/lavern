CLAIM

The Plaintiff claims:

  1. A declaration that the termination provision in the Plaintiff's employment agreement is void, invalid, and unenforceable;
  2. Damages for wrongful dismissal in the amount of $190,000, representing pay in lieu of common law reasonable notice, including base salary, bonus, and all other compensation and benefits through the reasonable notice period;
  3. In the alternative, the Plaintiff's minimum statutory entitlements under the Employment Standards Act, 2000, S.O. 2000, c. 41, including termination pay and severance pay;
  4. Prejudgment and postjudgment interest pursuant to sections 128 and 129 of the Courts of Justice Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. C.43;
  5. The costs of this proceeding, on a substantial indemnity basis, plus applicable HST; and
  6. Such further and other relief as this Honourable Court deems just.

FACTS

The Parties

1. The Plaintiff, Marcus Webb (the "Plaintiff"), is an individual who at all material times resided in Ontario and was employed by the Defendant.

2. The Defendant, Cadence Manufacturing Ltd (the "Defendant" or the "Company"), is an employer carrying on business in Ontario.

The Employment Relationship

3. The Plaintiff was employed by the Defendant from April 11, 2011 until the termination of his employment on May 15, 2026, a period of approximately 15 years and 1 month.

4. At the time of termination, the Plaintiff held the position of Plant Operations Manager, a senior managerial role.

5. At the time of termination, the Plaintiff was 54 years of age.

6. The Plaintiff's compensation consisted of a base salary of $128,000 per annum, together with an annual bonus of $15,000.

7. Throughout his employment, the Plaintiff performed his duties faithfully, competently, and to the satisfaction of the Defendant.

The Termination

8. On May 15, 2026, the Defendant terminated the Plaintiff's employment. The stated reason for the termination was restructuring.

9. The termination was without cause.

10. The Defendant failed to provide the Plaintiff with reasonable notice of termination, or pay in lieu thereof, as required at common law.

The Termination Clause

11. The Plaintiff's employment agreement contained a termination provision that purported to limit the Plaintiff's entitlements upon termination. That provision reads verbatim as follows:

"The Company may terminate your employment at any time without cause upon providing you with the minimum notice or pay in lieu required by the Employment Standards Act, 2000, and nothing more."

12. The termination provision is void, invalid, and unenforceable because it contravenes the minimum standards mandated by the Employment Standards Act, 2000 (the "ESA").

13. As the termination provision is void and unenforceable, the Plaintiff is entitled to common law reasonable notice of termination.

LEGAL BASIS

The Termination Clause is Invalid and Unenforceable

14. The Plaintiff pleads that the termination provision fails to comply with the ESA and is therefore void and unenforceable in its entirety. Where any part of a termination provision contravenes the ESA, the entire provision is unenforceable, and the plaintiff's entitlement to reasonable notice at common law is not displaced: Waksdale v Swegon North America Inc., 2020 ONCA 391.

15. The Plaintiff further pleads that the termination provision purports to limit his entitlements to bare ESA minimums ("and nothing more") and thereby fails to satisfy the requirements of the ESA, including the requirement to continue benefit plan contributions during the statutory notice period under section 61 and to provide severance pay under section 64.

16. The Plaintiff pleads and relies upon the ESA, in particular sections 5, 54, 57, 60, 61, and 64.

Wrongful Dismissal and Reasonable Notice

17. As the termination provision is unenforceable, the Plaintiff is entitled to reasonable notice of termination at common law, determined in accordance with the factors set out in Bardal v Globe & Mail Ltd., [1960] O.W.N. 253 (H.C.J.), namely the character of employment, the length of service, the age of the employee, and the availability of similar employment having regard to the experience, training, and qualifications of the employee.

18. Having regard to the Plaintiff's length of service of approximately 15 years, his senior managerial position as Plant Operations Manager, his age of 54, and the limited availability of comparable employment, the Plaintiff pleads that a reasonable notice period of 16 to 24 months is appropriate.

Entitlement to Bonus and Other Compensation Through the Notice Period

19. The Plaintiff pleads that his damages for wrongful dismissal include all compensation he would have earned during the reasonable notice period, including his annual bonus of $15,000, together with all benefits and other forms of compensation.

20. The Plaintiff pleads that damages for lost bonus and other compensation are recoverable as part of the common law notice entitlement, and that no term of the employment agreement or bonus plan unambiguously removes that entitlement: Matthews v Ocean Nutrition Canada Ltd., 2020 SCC 26.

Statutory Entitlements

21. In the alternative, and at minimum, the Plaintiff is entitled to his statutory entitlements under the ESA, including termination pay under section 57 and severance pay under section 64. The Defendant has failed to provide the Plaintiff with these statutory entitlements.

DAMAGES PARTICULARS

22. The Plaintiff claims damages as follows:

  1. ESA statutory notice (termination pay) — 8 weeks: $19,692;
  2. ESA statutory severance pay: $37,154;
  3. Common law reasonable notice — 16 to 24 months of base salary, bonus, and benefits: $170,667 to $256,000, inclusive of and not in addition to the statutory amounts above.

23. The Plaintiff limits the total amount claimed in this action to $190,000, exclusive of interest and costs.

24. The Plaintiff pleads that the amount claimed falls within the monetary jurisdiction of the Simplified Procedure under Rule 76.

RULE 76 STATEMENT

This action is brought under the Simplified Procedure provided for in Rule 76 of the Rules of Civil Procedure. The Plaintiff states that the total amount claimed does not exceed $200,000, exclusive of interest and costs, and that this proceeding is properly brought under Rule 76.

3. Protected Ground(s)

The applicant relies on the following protected ground under section 5 of the Human Rights Code:

  1. Disability (as defined in section 10(1) of the Code, which includes a mental disorder). The applicant lives with a diagnosed anxiety disorder.

4. Social Area

Employment — the discrimination arises in the context of the applicant's employment with the respondent, contrary to section 5(1) of the Code.

5. Summary of the Complaint

  1. The applicant, Aisha Osei, was employed by the respondent, Brightpath Financial Group Inc, from 3 September 2019 until the end of her employment on 20 April 2026.
  2. At the material time, the applicant held the position of Senior Client Advisor.
  3. The applicant's compensation consisted of an annual base salary of $92,000, together with commissions totalling approximately $22,000.
  4. The applicant lives with an anxiety disorder, which is a disability within the meaning of section 10(1) of the Code.
  5. The applicant disclosed her anxiety disorder to the respondent and requested a gradual return-to-work schedule as an accommodation of her disability.
  6. Specifically, the applicant made a written request for a four-day work week during the first month of her return to work.
  7. The respondent denied the accommodation request by email, without any discussion or engagement in the accommodation process.
  8. Adverse treatment of the applicant began approximately two weeks after she disclosed her anxiety disorder and requested a gradual return-to-work schedule.
  9. As a result of the adverse treatment and the failure to accommodate, the applicant's employment ended on 20 April 2026 by way of constructive dismissal.

6. How the Respondent Discriminated

  1. The respondent discriminated against the applicant with respect to employment on the basis of disability, contrary to section 5(1) of the Code.
  2. The temporal connection between the applicant's disclosure of her anxiety disorder and the onset of adverse treatment — approximately two weeks — supports the inference that the applicant's disability was a factor in her adverse treatment.
  3. The respondent breached its procedural and substantive duty to accommodate the applicant's disability to the point of undue hardship. The respondent denied the applicant's written request for a four-day work week during her first month back by email and without any discussion, thereby failing to engage in the individualized accommodation inquiry required under the Code.
  4. The cumulative effect of the adverse treatment and the denial of accommodation rendered the applicant's continued employment untenable and amounted to a constructive dismissal effective 20 April 2026.
  5. The conduct engages the duty to accommodate recognized under the Code and the principles governing disability-related accommodation in employment.

7. Remedy Sought

The applicant seeks the following remedies pursuant to section 45.2 of the Code:

  1. Monetary compensation for injury to dignity, feelings, and self-respect arising from the discriminatory treatment and failure to accommodate, in an amount to be determined by the Tribunal but reflecting the serious nature of the conduct.
  2. Compensation for lost wages and benefits flowing from the constructive dismissal, including lost base salary of $92,000 per year and commissions of approximately $22,000 per year, together with lost benefits.
  3. Reinstatement to the position of Senior Client Advisor, if appropriate and sought.
  4. Policy changes and mandatory training requiring the respondent to develop and implement a disability accommodation and return-to-work policy, and to train management and human resources personnel on the duty to accommodate under the Code.
  5. Public interest remedies to promote compliance with the Code and prevent similar conduct in the future.
  6. The applicant claims total compensation in the amount of $120,000 CAD, exclusive of statutory entitlements pursued in another forum as described in Part 8.

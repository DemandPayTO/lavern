Legal Analysis

1. Wrongful Dismissal — Common Law Reasonable Notice

Absent an enforceable termination provision limiting our client's entitlements, an employee dismissed without cause is entitled to reasonable notice of termination at common law, or pay in lieu thereof. The applicable factors were established in Bardal v Globe & Mail, [1960] OJ No 149, and include the character of the employment, the length of service, the age of the employee, and the availability of similar employment having regard to the experience, training, and qualifications of the employee.

Applying the Bardal factors to our client's circumstances — including their managerial position as Assistant Manager, their length of service, and their age at termination — we assess our client's reasonable notice entitlement in the range of two to three months. Should Orchard Lane Bistro Ltd seek to rely on any written termination clause to limit these entitlements, we put you on notice that such clauses are frequently found to be unenforceable. In Waksdale v Swegon North America Inc, 2020 ONCA 391, the Court of Appeal held that where any part of the termination provisions of an employment agreement violates the Employment Standards Act, 2000, the entire termination scheme is void and the employee is entitled to common law reasonable notice. Similarly, in Machtinger v HOJ Industries Ltd, [1992] 1 SCR 986, the Supreme Court of Canada held that a termination clause purporting to provide less than the statutory minimum is void and unenforceable.

2. Statutory Entitlements under the Employment Standards Act, 2000

Independent of common law notice, our client is entitled to statutory notice of termination pursuant to section 57 of the Employment Standards Act, 2000. Given our client's length of service of more than one year but less than three years, our client is entitled to a minimum of one week of notice or pay in lieu thereof, amounting to $1,000.

With respect to statutory severance pay under section 64 of the Employment Standards Act, 2000, our client does not meet the threshold of five or more years of service and accordingly no severance pay is owing under that provision.

We note that statutory entitlements represent a floor, not a ceiling, and are inclusive of, rather than in addition to, our client's common law reasonable notice entitlement.

Damages Quantification

Our client's entitlements are particularised as follows:

  1. ESA statutory notice pay (section 57): $1,000;
  2. ESA statutory severance pay (section 64): $0 (service threshold not met);
  3. Common law reasonable notice (inclusive of statutory notice): two to three months, being $8,667 to $13,000.

OVERVIEW

Dana Woo is a 52 year old Operations Manager who worked for Meridian Logistics Inc. for nearly thirteen years before being terminated on 27 February 2026. This is a without cause wrongful dismissal case: the central question is the length of the reasonable notice period and whether the plaintiff is entitled to the bonus that would have accrued during that period. Meridian's offer of twelve weeks falls well short of both the common law range and, on the plaintiff's analysis, the statutory floor.

FACTUAL BACKGROUND

Dana Woo commenced employment with Meridian Logistics Inc. on 4 March 2013. Over the course of the relationship the plaintiff advanced to the position of Operations Manager, a supervisory role carrying responsibility for the employer's operational function.

At the date of termination the plaintiff earned an annual base salary of $104,000, together with an annual bonus of $9,000. As set out in the profile table above, the plaintiff was 52 years old and had approximately thirteen years of service.

On 27 February 2026, Meridian terminated the plaintiff's employment. The termination was without cause; no allegation of misconduct or performance deficiency has been raised.

Following termination, Meridian offered twelve weeks of compensation, payable as a lump sum. [LAWYER: confirm whether ESA statutory entitlements were paid separately or were purported to be included in the twelve week offer, and whether benefits were continued through any period.]

ISSUES IN DISPUTE

1. Reasonable notice period. The plaintiff's position is that a 52 year old managerial employee with nearly thirteen years of service, in the current labour market, falls squarely within the upper portion of the comparable range set out above, being 14 to 21 months. The defence will likely argue for the lower end, emphasizing that the plaintiff's role, while managerial, was not senior executive, and that comparable positions in logistics remain available. The comparables above address this contest and support notice materially above the twelve weeks offered.

2. Statutory entitlements as a floor. On the plaintiff's calculation the ESA alone yields eight weeks' notice ($16,000) plus severance pay of $25,971, as set out in the damages table. That combined statutory floor exceeds the twelve week offer. If Meridian's twelve weeks was intended to satisfy both notice and severance obligations, the offer does not meet the statutory minimum, which is a significant credibility problem for the defence. [LAWYER: confirm Meridian's Ontario payroll exceeded $2.5 million so that ESA severance is engaged; this drives the $25,971 figure.]

3. Entitlement to the bonus over the notice period (Matthews). The plaintiff seeks the value of the $9,000 annual bonus as a component of common law damages over the notice period. The live question is whether any bonus plan language unambiguously and enforceably excludes entitlement on termination. The defence may point to plan wording requiring active employment at the payout date. The plaintiff's position is that such language, absent clear and enforceable limiting terms, does not defeat a claim for the bonus the plaintiff would have earned had reasonable notice been given. [LAWYER: obtain the bonus plan document and any signed acknowledgement; the enforceability analysis turns entirely on that text.]

RESPONSE TO ANTICIPATED DEFENCES

Written termination clause. The most likely defence is reliance on a contractual termination provision limiting the plaintiff to something at or near the twelve week offer. [LAWYER: obtain and scrutinize the employment contract.] The plaintiff anticipates challenging enforceability on the now familiar grounds that a termination clause which offends the ESA in any respect, including the treatment of severance, benefits continuation, or the "without cause" and "with cause" provisions read together, is void in its entirety. Given that the offer appears to fall below the statutory floor, there is a real question whether any such clause was honoured on its own terms, which further undermines reliance on it.

Low end of the notice range. The defence will urge the shorter end of the comparable range. The plaintiff acknowledges that a managerial, non executive role and any re employment prospects in logistics are relevant factors that temper the claim. That candour is reflected in the plaintiff's realistic range below, which does not insist on the 21 month ceiling.

No just cause is alleged, and the plaintiff proceeds on that basis. Should any conduct allegation emerge, the plaintiff reserves the right to respond, but there is nothing on the present record to support cause.

SETTLEMENT POSITION

The litigation alternative favours resolution now. If this matter proceeds, both sides face documentary and oral discovery, likely a motion or trial on the enforceability of the termination clause and the bonus plan, and the associated delay and cost. A defence that relies on a clause paying below the ESA floor carries meaningful risk on both liability for notice and on costs.

The statutory entitlements set the floor: ESA notice of $16,000 plus severance of $25,971, totalling $41,971, is owed regardless of outcome. Above that floor, the common law range of 14 to 21 months (approximately $121,333 to $182,000 as set out in the damages table), inclusive of the bonus claim, frames the exposure.

Recognizing litigation risk on both sides, the plaintiff's realistic settlement range corresponds to notice in the range of roughly 16 to 18 months, inclusive of the bonus component and net of anticipated mitigation, rather than the ceiling. As reflected in the negotiation history above, there have been no substantive negotiations to date; the twelve week offer is the only figure exchanged and is not a meaningful starting point. The plaintiff comes to mediation prepared to move toward a reasonable resolution if Meridian engages realistically.

MEDIATION OBJECTIVES

The plaintiff seeks the following:

  1. A monetary settlement within the realistic range described above, satisfying the statutory floor at minimum;
  2. A positive letter of reference confirming title, tenure, and duties;
  3. Continuation of benefits for a defined period or compensation in lieu;
  4. A mutual non disparagement clause;
  5. Tax efficient structuring of the settlement, including allocation to a retiring allowance and RRSP transfer where available, and separate treatment of any general damages component.

PRACTICAL CONSIDERATIONS

The plaintiff values closure. At 52, with a mortgage and household obligations to consider, prolonged litigation carries real personal cost, and a prompt, certain resolution has genuine value. Meridian, for its part, faces cost exposure and reputational considerations if the enforceability and statutory floor arguments are tested.

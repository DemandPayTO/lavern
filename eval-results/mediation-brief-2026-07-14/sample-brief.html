OVERVIEW

1.  Dana Woo is a 52-year-old Operations Manager who gave Meridian Logistics Inc. nearly thirteen years of service before being dismissed on February 27, 2026. This is a wrongful dismissal case about reasonable notice for a long-service, mid-senior employee, together with the plaintiff's statutory severance and entitlement to the bonus that would have accrued during the notice period. The defendant's offer of twelve weeks falls well short of both the common law range and, importantly, the plaintiff's statutory floor.

ISSUES IN DISPUTE

7.  1. Reasonable notice. The plaintiff's position is that a 52-year-old Operations Manager with nearly thirteen years of service falls squarely within the 14 to 21 month range reflected in the comparable cases set out above and in the damages table. The defendant may argue for the lower end, emphasizing that the role, while managerial, was mid-tier rather than executive, and that comparable positions in the logistics sector remain available. The plaintiff's age and length of service push meaningfully toward the middle of the range.

8.  2. Statutory severance and the twelve-week offer. This is the plaintiff's clearest point. With nearly thirteen years of service, Dana Woo is entitled under the Employment Standards Act, 2000 to eight weeks of termination pay plus severance pay, which together substantially exceed twelve weeks in value, as set out in the damages table. A twelve-week offer that does not clearly exceed the statutory floor is, on its face, deficient. [LAWYER: confirm whether Meridian's Ontario payroll exceeds $2.5 million so that ESA severance is engaged; the damages table assumes it is.]

9.  3. Bonus during the notice period (Matthews). The plaintiff seeks the bonus that would have been earned over the notice period. The defendant may point to language in any bonus or RSU plan requiring active employment at the payout date. The contested question is whether such language is sufficiently clear and unambiguous to displace the common law presumption that damages include amounts that would have accrued during the notice period. Absent clear exclusionary wording, the bonus forms part of the damages. [LAWYER: the strength of this claim turns entirely on the plan wording, which is not yet in the record.]

SETTLEMENT POSITION

15.  The litigation alternative is not attractive for either party. If this matter does not resolve, the parties face examinations for discovery, documentary production of the bonus and RSU plans and payroll records, and likely a motion for summary judgment, a procedure well suited to a straightforward wrongful dismissal claim. That path involves months of delay and meaningful legal costs on both sides, with cost consequences that favour a plaintiff who beats a reasonable offer. The plaintiff's damages are largely a matter of arithmetic once the notice period is fixed.

16.  The statutory entitlement (eight weeks of termination pay plus severance, as itemized in the damages table) is the floor: it is owed regardless of the notice analysis and already exceeds the twelve-week offer. Building from that floor, the common law claim supports damages in the 14 to 21 month range, and the plaintiff's realistic settlement expectation sits in the middle of that range, adjusted for mitigation and the bonus claim as the plan documents are clarified.

17.  The negotiation history above reflects only Meridian's twelve-week offer; there has been no meaningful exchange of positions. We are prepared to negotiate in good faith and to move from our opening position in response to genuine engagement.

MEDIATION OBJECTIVES

18.  The plaintiff seeks the following:

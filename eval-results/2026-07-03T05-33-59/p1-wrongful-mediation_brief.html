1. Nature of the Action

This is an action for wrongful dismissal. The Plaintiff, Marcus Webb, was employed by the Defendant, Cadence Manufacturing Ltd, as Plant Operations Manager from 11 April 2011 until termination on 15 May 2026, a period of approximately 15 years. The Defendant terminated the Plaintiff's employment on a stated basis of restructuring. The Plaintiff claims that any termination provision in the employment contract is unenforceable and seeks reasonable notice at common law, together with damages relating to a bonus entitlement.

2. Factual Background

  1. The Plaintiff commenced employment with the Defendant on 11 April 2011.
  2. At the time of termination, the Plaintiff held the position of Plant Operations Manager.
  3. The Plaintiff was 54 years of age at the date of termination.
  4. The Plaintiff's compensation consisted of an annual base salary of $128,000, together with a bonus of $15,000.
  5. On 15 May 2026, the Defendant terminated the Plaintiff's employment, citing restructuring as the reason.
  6. The Plaintiff's total length of service was approximately 15 years and one month.

5. Legal Issues

  1. Reasonable notice (Bardal v. Globe & Mail Ltd.): Reasonable notice is assessed on character of employment, length of service, age, and availability of similar employment. The Plaintiff's 15 years of service, age of 54, and senior managerial role support a substantial notice period.
  2. Termination clause invalidity (Machtinger v. HOJ Industries): A termination provision that contracts out of ESA minimums is void, and the presumption of reasonable notice at common law revives.
  3. Waksdale v. Swegon North America Inc.: Where a "with cause" provision violates the ESA, the entire termination clause is unenforceable, notwithstanding a severability clause or an otherwise compliant "without cause" provision.
  4. Bonus entitlement (Matthews v. Ocean Nutrition Canada Ltd.): An employee is entitled to bonus compensation that would have accrued during the reasonable notice period unless the plan language unambiguously and enforceably removes that common law entitlement.
  5. Bad faith / Honda Canada Inc. v. Keays: Reserved. No aggravated or moral damages are pleaded at this stage; the manner of dismissal will be assessed pending disclosure.
  6. ESA severance pay: An employee with five or more years of service where the employer meets the payroll threshold is entitled to statutory severance in addition to notice.

7. Settlement Range

Best case: If the termination clause is found void on both the Machtinger and Waksdale grounds, and the notice period is fixed at the higher end (24 months), the Plaintiff's damages including bonus would approach $256,000–$286,000, less mitigation.

Likely range: The most probable outcome is a notice period of 17–20 months, inclusive of bonus, yielding a gross award in the range of $195,000–$240,000 before mitigation. A realistic negotiated settlement falls in the range of $150,000–$190,000, reflecting litigation risk and mitigation uncertainty.

Floor: The minimum acceptable outcome is the Plaintiff's ESA baseline: 8 weeks' notice ($19,692) plus severance pay ($37,154), totalling approximately $56,846. The Plaintiff will not settle below this statutory baseline.

8. Mediation Objectives

  1. A lump-sum monetary settlement within the likely range identified above.
  2. A positive letter of reference confirming the Plaintiff's title, tenure, and good standing.
  3. Payment or continuation of benefits, or a cash equivalent, over a portion of the notice period.
  4. Confirmation of the bonus entitlement or its inclusion in the settlement figure.
  5. A mutual non-disparagement clause.
  6. Favourable tax structuring of the settlement where lawful (e.g., allocation to retiring allowance/RRSP where available).

9. Practical Considerations

  1. Candid assessment of weaknesses: The strength of the Waksdale argument depends on the actual contractual language, which will be central. If the clause is well-drafted and ESA-compliant, the Plaintiff's recovery may be limited toward the statutory floor. This risk should temper expectations.
  2. Mitigation: The Plaintiff has a duty to seek comparable employment. Given the Plaintiff's age (54) and senior specialised role, re-employment may take time, which supports the notice claim; however, any income earned reduces damages, and the defence will scrutinise the mitigation record.
  3. Bonus characterisation: The bonus recovery turns on the plan documentation. If the plan contains enforceable forfeiture language, the Matthews claim may be reduced.
  4. Cost exposure: Proceeding to trial carries significant cost risk for both parties. Early resolution avoids substantial legal fees and disbursements.
  5. Emotional toll and closure: The Plaintiff, at a later career stage, values certainty and a clean resolution that preserves reputation and facilitates re-employment.
  6. Limitation and timing: The action was commenced within the applicable limitation period. Prompt resolution serves the Plaintiff's interest in financial stability during the transition.

CLAIM

The Plaintiff claims:

  1. Damages in the amount of $190,000 for wrongful dismissal, representing pay in lieu of common law reasonable notice, inclusive of base salary, bonus, and all other compensation and benefits that would have accrued during the reasonable notice period;
  2. A declaration that the termination provision in the Plaintiff's employment agreement is void, invalid, and unenforceable;
  3. Payment of the Plaintiff's minimum statutory entitlements under the Employment Standards Act, 2000, S.O. 2000, c. 41, including termination pay and severance pay, to the extent not otherwise recovered;
  4. Damages for the loss of the Plaintiff's annual bonus through the reasonable notice period;
  5. Pre-judgment and post-judgment interest pursuant to sections 128 and 129 of the Courts of Justice Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. C.43;
  6. The costs of this proceeding on a substantial indemnity basis, plus applicable HST; and
  7. Such further and other relief as this Honourable Court may deem just.

FACTS

The Parties

1. The Plaintiff, Marcus Webb (the "Plaintiff"), is an individual who resides in the Province of Ontario and who was, at all material times, employed by the Defendant.

2. The Defendant, Cadence Manufacturing Ltd (the "Defendant" or the "Company"), is an employer carrying on business in the Province of Ontario.

The Employment Relationship

3. The Plaintiff commenced employment with the Defendant on April 11, 2011.

4. At the date of termination, the Plaintiff held the position of Plant Operations Manager, a senior managerial position.

5. The Plaintiff was employed by the Defendant continuously for a period of approximately 15 years and 1 month.

6. At the date of termination, the Plaintiff was 54 years of age.

7. The Plaintiff's compensation at the date of termination consisted of an annual base salary of $128,000, together with an annual bonus of $15,000. The Plaintiff's total annual compensation was therefore approximately $143,000.

8. Throughout his employment, the Plaintiff performed his duties competently, faithfully, and to the satisfaction of the Defendant.

The Termination Clause

9. The Plaintiff's employment was governed by a written employment agreement (the "Employment Agreement") which contained a termination provision (the "Termination Clause").

10. The Termination Clause provided, verbatim, as follows:

"The Company may terminate your employment at any time without cause upon providing you with the minimum notice or pay in lieu required by the Employment Standards Act, 2000, and nothing more."

The Termination of Employment

11. On or about May 15, 2026, the Defendant terminated the Plaintiff's employment.

12. The Defendant stated that the reason for the termination was restructuring.

13. The termination was without cause.

14. The Defendant has failed to provide the Plaintiff with reasonable notice of the termination of his employment, or pay in lieu thereof.

15. The Defendant has further failed to provide the Plaintiff with his full statutory entitlements under the Employment Standards Act, 2000, including termination pay and severance pay.

LEGAL BASIS

The Termination Clause is Invalid and Unenforceable

16. The Plaintiff pleads that the Termination Clause is void, invalid, and unenforceable, and that he is therefore entitled to common law reasonable notice of the termination of his employment.

17. The Termination Clause purports to limit the Plaintiff's entitlement on termination to "the minimum notice or pay in lieu required by the Employment Standards Act, 2000, and nothing more."

18. The Termination Clause fails to provide for the Plaintiff's statutory entitlement to severance pay under section 64 of the Employment Standards Act, 2000, and fails to provide for the continuation of benefits during the statutory notice period as required by section 61 of the Act. It therefore contracts out of the Plaintiff's statutory entitlements contrary to section 5(1) of the Employment Standards Act, 2000.

19. The Termination Clause is further void because, when read as a whole, the termination provisions of the Employment Agreement violate the Employment Standards Act, 2000. As held in Waksdale v. Swegon North America Inc., 2020 ONCA 391, where any part of the termination provisions of an employment agreement offends the Employment Standards Act, 2000, the entire termination scheme is void and unenforceable, and severability provisions cannot save it.

20. As the Termination Clause is unenforceable, the presumption of common law reasonable notice is not rebutted, and the Plaintiff is entitled to damages for wrongful dismissal based upon common law reasonable notice.

Wrongful Dismissal and Reasonable Notice

21. The Plaintiff is entitled to common law reasonable notice of the termination of his employment, having regard to the factors set out in Bardal v. Globe & Mail Ltd. (1960), 24 D.L.R. (2d) 140 (Ont. H.C.), namely:

  1. the character of the Plaintiff's employment as a senior Plant Operations Manager;
  2. the Plaintiff's length of service of approximately 15 years and 1 month;
  3. the Plaintiff's age of 54 years at the date of termination; and
  4. the availability of similar employment, having regard to the Plaintiff's experience, training, and qualifications.

22. Having regard to the Bardal factors, the Plaintiff pleads that a reasonable notice period in the range of 16 to 24 months is appropriate.

Entitlement to Bonus Through the Notice Period

23. The Plaintiff is entitled to damages for the loss of his annual bonus over the course of the reasonable notice period. As held in Matthews v. Ocean Nutrition Canada Ltd., 2020 SCC 26, an employee is entitled to damages reflecting the compensation and benefits, including bonus entitlements, that would have been earned during the reasonable notice period, absent clear and unambiguous language removing that entitlement.

24. There is no language in the Employment Agreement that clearly and unambiguously removes the Plaintiff's common law entitlement to the bonus during the reasonable notice period.

Statutory Entitlements

25. The Plaintiff pleads and relies upon the Employment Standards Act, 2000, S.O. 2000, c. 41, and in particular sections 5, 54, 57, 60, 61, and 64 thereof.

DAMAGES PARTICULARS

26. The Plaintiff claims damages as particularized below:

  1. Statutory termination pay (8 weeks) under section 57 of the Employment Standards Act, 2000: $19,692;
  2. Statutory severance pay under section 64 of the Employment Standards Act, 2000: $37,154;
  3. Common law reasonable notice (16 to 24 months), inclusive of base salary, bonus, and benefits, in the range of $170,667 to $256,000, subject to the monetary limit of the Simplified Procedure;
  4. Loss of annual bonus through the reasonable notice period, in accordance with Matthews v. Ocean Nutrition Canada Ltd., 2020 SCC 26.

27. The Plaintiff pleads that his statutory entitlements are subsumed within, and are not in addition to, the common law reasonable notice damages, save to the extent that the statutory severance pay is a distinct minimum entitlement.

28. The Plaintiff limits his total claim in this proceeding to $190,000, exclusive of interest and costs, in accordance with the monetary jurisdiction of the Simplified Procedure under Rule 76.

29. The Plaintiff has taken and will continue to take reasonable steps to mitigate his damages by seeking comparable alternative employment.

Employer: Trillium Paper Products Inc (Cornwall mill)

Nature of grievance: Discharge

1. Overview

The grievor, Claude Boisvert, is a Millwright in the Maintenance Department with over 18 years of continuous, essentially unblemished service, having commenced employment on 17 March 2008. On 24 June 2026 the Employer alleged that the grievor falsified a lockout verification record on a single occasion, and on 26 June 2026 discharged him for "falsification of safety documentation." No investigation was conducted before the discipline was imposed, the grievor was afforded no union representation, and the discharge meeting lasted four minutes. The Union submits the Employer lacked just cause, that the discharge was excessively disproportionate to any proven misconduct, and that the process was fundamentally defective. The Union seeks reinstatement with full back pay (estimated at $96,000), benefits, pension, and seniority, together with interest.

2. The Parties and the Collective Agreement

The Union is the exclusive bargaining agent for the bargaining unit in which the grievor is employed at the Employer's Cornwall mill. The parties are bound by the 2023–2026 Collective Agreement.

The grievance procedure is set out in Article 11 of the Collective Agreement. Time limits within the grievance procedure are stated to be mandatory, and the deadline for filing this discharge grievance is ten (10) working days, which the Union calculates to be 8 July 2026.

Discipline and discharge are governed by the just-cause standard in Article 5.02. Where the Employer disciplines or discharges an employee, the onus rests on the Employer to establish just cause.   2. The grievor's seniority date is 17 March 2008. As at the date of discharge, he had approximately 18 years of continuous service.

  8. The grievor was not afforded union representation at the disciplinary meeting.
  9. The disciplinary meeting lasted four minutes.
  10. By letter dated 26 June 2026, the Employer discharged the grievor. The Employer's stated ground was "falsification of safety documentation."
  11. The grievance filing deadline under Article 11 is 8 July 2026 (ten working days). Counsel must confirm that the grievance is filed within this mandatory time limit.

4. Issues

  1. Was the grievance filed within the mandatory time limits under Article 11, and is the matter properly and exclusively before this Board of Arbitration?
  2. Did the Employer have just cause to discharge the grievor?
  3. If some misconduct is established, was discharge an excessive penalty; and if so, what lesser penalty (if any) should be substituted?
  4. Did the Employer's failure to investigate, to afford union representation, and to conduct a meaningful disciplinary meeting constitute procedural defects that void or undermine the discipline?
  5. May the Employer rely on the grievor's prior disciplinary record?
  6. What is the appropriate remedy?

5. Argument

A. Jurisdiction and Time Limits (weber_exclusive_forum; grievance_time_limits)

This dispute arises expressly out of the interpretation, application and administration of the Collective Agreement — specifically the just-cause protection in Article 5.02 and the disciplinary provisions of the Collective Agreement. Under the principle affirmed in Weber v. Ontario Hydro, [1995] 2 S.C.R. 929, where the essential character of a dispute arises from the collective agreement, arbitration is the exclusive forum for its resolution. The discharge of the grievor is a quintessential collective agreement dispute, and this Board has exclusive jurisdiction to determine it and to grant a full make-whole remedy.

The Union is alive to the mandatory nature of the time limits in Article 11. Because the grievance had not been filed as of the date of these instructions, the Union's first imperative is to ensure the discharge grievance is filed on or before the mandatory deadline of 8 July 2026. Counsel must confirm timely filing; failure to do so risks a preliminary objection to arbitrability. The Union will resist any such objection and, where necessary, will rely on the Board's discretion to relieve against time limits under section 48(16) of the Labour Relations Act, 1995, which empowers an arbitrator to extend time limits notwithstanding that they are described as mandatory, where there are reasonable grounds and no substantial prejudice.

B. The Employer Did Not Have Just Cause (just_cause_challenge)

The onus rests squarely on the Employer to prove, on a balance of probabilities and by clear and cogent evidence, that the misconduct occurred and that it warranted discharge. An allegation of falsification of a safety document is an allegation of dishonesty and, given its gravity and consequences for the grievor's reputation and livelihood, requires particularly clear and cogent evidence.

The analysis is governed by the well-established framework in Re United Steelworkers of America, Local 337 and Wm. Scott & Company Ltd., [1977] 1 Can. L.R.B.R. 1 (B.C.L.R.B.), which asks three questions:

  1. Was there just cause for some discipline? The Employer alleges a single instance of falsifying a lockout verification record. The Union puts the Employer to the strict proof of the underlying conduct, including whether the record was in fact false, whether the grievor made the entry, and whether the grievor acted with any dishonest intent as opposed to inadvertence, error, or a good-faith understanding of the verification steps taken. A single documentary discrepancy, without proof of deliberate falsification, does not establish culpable misconduct.
  2. If so, was discharge an excessive response? Even if some misconduct is established, discharge of an 18-year employee for a single alleged incident is grossly disproportionate. Discharge is the capital penalty of industrial relations and must be reserved for the most serious cases or for the culmination of a documented disciplinary history. Here there is neither.
  3. If excessive, what lesser penalty should be substituted? The Board has full remedial authority to substitute a penalty that is just and reasonable. Given the grievor's long, clean service, the Union submits that reinstatement without loss is warranted; in the alternative, any penalty short of discharge.

Proportionality and mitigating factors. The grievor's 18 years of service is the dominant mitigating factor. Long service is consistently treated in the arbitral jurisprudence as a powerful ground for substituting a lesser penalty, particularly where the record is otherwise clean. The alleged conduct is a single, isolated occasion. There is no evidence of a pattern, no evidence of harm resulting, and no evidence that the grievor was warned or given any opportunity to correct or explain. On any proportionality analysis, discharge cannot stand.

C. Fatal Procedural Defects (procedural_defects)

The manner in which the discharge was carried out was fundamentally unfair and offends basic principles of industrial due process:

  1. No investigation. The Employer conducted no investigation before imposing the ultimate penalty. An employer cannot fairly conclude that an employee acted dishonestly without first investigating the facts and affording the employee a genuine opportunity to respond. The absence of any investigation deprives the Employer's conclusion of any reliable evidentiary foundation.
  2. No union representation. The grievor was denied union representation at the disciplinary meeting. This deprived him of the assistance of the bargaining agent at the most consequential moment of his employment.
  3. A four-minute meeting. A discharge meeting lasting four minutes demonstrates that the outcome was predetermined and that the grievor was afforded no meaningful opportunity to give his account before the decision was made. The brevity of the meeting is itself compelling evidence that the Employer did not engage in any good-faith consideration of the grievor's explanation or of alternatives to discharge.

These defects, individually and cumulatively, weigh heavily against the discipline. Where procedural fairness is denied and no investigation precedes discharge, arbitrators may void the discipline or treat the defects as a significant factor favouring reinstatement and substitution of penalty.

D. The Employer Cannot Rely on the Prior Record (prior_record_challenge)

The grievor's only prior discipline is a written warning issued in 2019 for late reporting. The Collective Agreement contains a 24-month sunset clause. The 2019 warning is spent and must be disregarded entirely; it cannot form part of any progressive discipline analysis and cannot be used to bolster the penalty. Effectively, the grievor stands before this Board with an unblemished record over 18 years of service. Moreover, the nature of the spent warning — late reporting — bears no relationship to the alleged conduct here and would carry no weight in any event.

E. Duty of Fair Representation Considerations (dfr_exposure)

The Union notes for internal purposes that, because the grievance had not yet been filed and a mandatory ten-working-day time limit expires on 8 July 2026, timely and diligent processing of this grievance is essential to protect both the grievor's rights and the Union against any duty of fair representation exposure under the Labour Relations Act, 1995. This factor reinforces the necessity of filing without delay, advancing the grievance vigorously, and preserving the arbitrability arguments set out above.

6. Remedy (reinstatement_make_whole)

The presumptive remedy for a discharge found to be without just cause is reinstatement with full make-whole compensation. The Union seeks:

  1. Reinstatement of the grievor to his position as Millwright in the Maintenance Department, without loss of seniority (with continuous seniority from 17 March 2008);
  2. Full back pay from the date of discharge to the date of reinstatement, estimated at approximately $96,000, subject to adjustment;
  3. Restoration and compensation for all lost benefits and pension contributions/credits;
  4. Interest on all monetary amounts;
  5. Removal of all reference to the discharge and the underlying allegation from the grievor's record.

In the alternative, if the Board finds some misconduct but that discharge was excessive, the Union asks the Board to exercise its authority to substitute a lesser penalty and to order corresponding compensation for the period of any excess penalty.

7. Order Requested

  1. A declaration that the Employer did not have just cause to discharge the grievor;
  2. An order reinstating the grievor to his position without loss of seniority or service;
  3. An order for full back pay, estimated at $96,000, subject to proof;
  4. An order restoring and compensating all lost benefits and pension entitlements;
  5. An order for interest on all monetary amounts;
  6. An order that all record of the discharge and the underlying allegation be expunged from the grievor's file;
  7. In the alternative, an order substituting a penalty less than discharge and compensating the grievor accordingly;
  8. An order that the Board remain seised with respect to the implementation and quantification of the remedy.
